## Overview

A **volcano** is a structure formed where magma, lava and pyroclastic material reach the surface, and most lie along plate boundaries. Volcanoes are hazards and also sources of geothermal power, and they have shaped large parts of the world's terrain and soils.

## Distribution and numbers

About $1350$ volcanoes are known worldwide, and the great majority lie along the Circum-Pacific belt, which holds more than three quarters of the world's active volcanoes. Divergent boundaries produce ridge volcanoes, subduction zones build arcs such as the Andes, and intraplate settings give rise to hotspot volcanoes like Hawaii. Northern Taiwan's Datun volcanic group formed in a back-arc extensional setting.

## Eruption styles and scale

By products and shape, volcanoes are grouped into shield volcanoes, composite volcanoes and grouped complexes. The Volcanic Explosivity Index runs from $0$ to $8$, and events of VEI $4$ and above generate substantial pyroclastic material. The largest known eruption was that of Toba about $74000$ years ago, which expelled roughly $2800$ cubic kilometres of pyroclastic material.

| Type | Build | Example | Character |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shield | Slow lava effusion | Kilauea, Hawaii | Gentle slopes, basaltic |
| Composite | Alternating lava and tephra | Mount Fuji | Steep cone, explosive |
| Pyroclastic shield | Rapid tephra accumulation | Some Pleistocene cones | Flat-topped |
| Submarine | Eruption below sea level | Mid-ocean ridges | Builds new ocean crust |

## Monitoring and hazards

Modern monitoring rests mainly on seismology, combined with tiltmeter deformation, gas measurements and thermal imaging of crater changes, allowing warnings days to months ahead. The deadliest products are often not lava but lahars, pyroclastic flows and sector collapse: the $1980$ eruption of Mount St Helens was modest in scale yet killed $57$ people and caused billions of dollars in losses.

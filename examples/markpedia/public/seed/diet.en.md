## Overview

A **diet** is the pattern and the amounts of food taken in over time. The effect of any single nutrient is usually smaller than that of the overall pattern, while portions, ratios and the degree of processing together determine how much nutrition each calorie carries.

## Dietary patterns and chronic disease

The Mediterranean pattern centres on whole grains, vegetables, fruit, olive oil and fish, and several large studies report improvements in cardiovascular events and metabolic markers. The DASH pattern lowers systolic blood pressure by roughly $5$ to $8$ mmHg in hypertensive adults, an effect comparable to a low dose of antihypertensive drug. Controlling total energy and holding body weight steady is the shared foundation of all patterns, and the main route by which chronic disease risk falls.

## Portions and plate proportions

Taiwan's health promotion agency teaches portions with a 'healthy plate' slogan: fruit the size of a fist at each meal, vegetables a little more than the fruit, and rice equal in quantity to the vegetables, plus a recommendation to replace more than half the white rice with wholegrains. Recommended fruit and vegetables total $5$ portions a day, $3$ of vegetables and $2$ of fruit, and higher shares of grains, fruit and vegetables are generally associated with lower chronic disease risk.

| Pattern | Main components | Observed benefits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mediterranean | Whole grains, vegetables, fruit, olive oil, fish | Better cardiovascular and metabolic markers |
| DASH | Vegetables, fruit, low-fat dairy, whole grains, low sodium | Blood pressure falls $5$ to $8$ mmHg |
| Low carbohydrate | Less refined sugar and starch | Short-term weight and glucose reduction |
| Plant-based | Predominantly plant foods | Associated with lower cardiovascular risk |

## Components to limit

The World Health Organization recommends keeping **free sugars** below $5$ % of total energy, about $25$ g on a $2\,000$ kcal diet, to lower the risk of obesity and diabetes, and salt below $5$ g a day. Saturated fat should stay under $10$ % of energy and trans fats close to zero. Alcohol has no clear safe threshold, so less is better; by contrast, dietary fibre and calcium are what most people fall short on.

## Ultra-processed food

The NOVA scheme divides foods into four groups, the last of which is ultra-processed products containing more sugar, salt and fat with little fibre. In controlled trials, an ultra-processed diet adding roughly $500$ kcal a day for about two weeks produced close to $1$ kg of weight gain, while a diet of unprocessed food led to weight loss, and observational work finds slightly higher cancer risk among people drawing more energy from ultra-processed products.

## Overview

**Money** is a widely accepted medium of exchange that also serves as a unit of account and a store of value. Most modern money is credit-based, resting on trust in the issuer and the monetary system rather than on any intrinsic material.

## Functions and evolution

Money serves four functions: medium of exchange, unit of account, store of value and standard of deferred payment. Historically it moved through commodity money, metal money and credit money. Under the Bretton Woods system established in 1944, the dollar was pegged to gold at 35 US dollars per ounce; after the United States stopped converting in 1971, the world moved fully onto a credit basis.

## Money supply and the multiplier

Money aggregates are usually split into $ M_0 $ (currency in circulation), $ M_1 $ (currency plus demand deposits) and $ M_2 $ (adding savings and time deposits). Banks create deposits through lending, and the simple deposit multiplier is $ 1 / r $ — with a 10 % reserve ratio about 10. Allowing for currency leakage and excess reserves, the practical multiplier is often only 2 to 3.

## Forms and measures of money

| Form | Description | Key feature |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Currency in circulation | Notes and coins | Demand shifts with rates and payment habits |
| Bank deposits | Liabilities of commercial banks | The reserve ratio sets the multiplier |
| Electronic payments | E-money and third-party balances | Usually a liability rather than money itself |
| Reserve ratio | Fraction of deposits banks must hold | The key parameter in the multiplier $ 1 / r $ |

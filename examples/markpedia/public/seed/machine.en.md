## Overview

**Machine** is a device assembled from parts that have well-defined relative motion and that performs a specified function or transmits energy. The task of machine design is to take a demanded motion or power at the input and find a mechanism and set of components that meet it while remaining acceptable in efficiency, cost and service life.

## Components and classification

A complete machine normally contains a prime mover converting fuel or electricity into mechanical energy, a transmission system of belts, gears, shafts and bearings, and a working machine that performs the actual task. By energy role they split into prime movers and work machines: engines and electric motors on one side, cranes, pumps and machine tools on the other. The first yardstick for performance is efficiency, the ratio of output work to input work.

## Degrees of freedom and determinate motion

The first step in mechanism design is to count degrees of freedom and check that every member has uniquely determined motion. For planar mechanisms the Grübler criterion gives $ n = 3(m-1) - 2j - h $, where $ m $ is the number of links, $ j $ the number of lower pairs and $ h $ the number of higher pairs. A four-bar linkage has four links and four lower pairs, giving mobility $ 3 \times 3 - 2 \times 4 = 1 $.

## Efficiency and reference figures

Efficiency varies enormously between energy conversion paths and directly affects both energy use and thermal design. For unit conversions, $ 1 \ \mathrm{kW\cdot h} = 3.6 \ \mathrm{MJ} $ and $ 1 \ \mathrm{hp} \approx 746 \ \mathrm{W} $. The table below gives typical efficiency ranges for common devices.

| Device | Typical efficiency | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Petrol engine | 25 % – 35 % | Large losses through exhaust and cooling |
| Diesel engine | 35 % – 45 % | Higher compression ratio beats petrol |
| Induction motor | 85 % – 95 % | Simple construction, easy maintenance |
| Synchronous brushless motor | 90 % – 97 % | Needs position sensors and variable-frequency drive |
| Single-stage gear drive | 97 % – 99 % | Depends on lubrication and surface finish |
| Hydraulic drive | 80 % – 90 % | High power density, suited to heavy loads |

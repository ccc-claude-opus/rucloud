## Overview

**Health insurance** is a system that pools risk and compensates medical expenses, using contributions and benefits so that the many share the large costs of the few. Its performance depends on how the risk pool is designed, how providers are paid, and whether the finances hold together.

## How a risk pool works

The core of health insurance is the **risk pool**: most people pay premiums while only a minority receive care in any given period, so large medical costs are spread across the membership and no single family is ruined by one serious illness. Two classic problems must be managed: **adverse selection**, in which the highest-risk people are most likely to enrol, and **moral hazard**, in which people consume more care once costs are paid by others. Insurers respond with deductibles, co-payments and claims scrutiny.

## Payment methods

The way insurers pay providers shapes both clinical behaviour and spending growth. **Fee-for-service** pays for each item delivered and tends to induce unnecessary services; **diagnosis-related groups** (DRG/DIP) assign a fixed amount per disease group and reward shorter stays; **capitation** prepays per person and shifts the total financial risk onto the provider. These methods are not mutually exclusive, and most systems mix them to balance incentives and risk sharing.

| Payment method | Basis of payment | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fee-for-service | Each service item | Over-provision of services |
| DRG/DIP | Fixed amount per disease group | Selection of cases, compressed length of stay |
| Capitation | Prepayment per person | Under-spending on high-risk groups |
| Differential payment | Patient pays the difference | Cost shifting and access barriers |

## Structure of Taiwan's National Health Insurance

Taiwan's National Health Insurance took effect on 1 March 1995 and is administered by the National Health Insurance Administration under the Ministry of Health and Welfare. Coverage has stayed above $99$ %, with more than $23$ million people enrolled, making it one of the few systems with universal coverage. Premiums are shared by the insured, their employers and government, with subsidies for low-income households. The hospital admission rate runs near $9$ %, and the scheme targets a roughly balanced budget, yet ageing keeps pressure on benefits.

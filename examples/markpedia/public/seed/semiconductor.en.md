## Overview

**Semiconductor** is a material whose conductivity lies between that of metals and insulators and that can be tuned by doping and applied electric fields. Silicon is the dominant substrate material: through p-n junctions and transistors it forms the integrated circuits that let electronic devices process and store information at high speed and very low power, and it underlies nearly every modern electronic product.

## Band gaps and resistivity

Conduction in semiconductors is governed by the band structure: the smaller the band gap, the more easily electrons cross it. Silicon has a gap of about 1.12 eV, germanium 0.67 eV, gallium arsenide 1.42 eV, silicon carbide 3.26 eV and gallium nitride 3.4 eV. Intrinsic silicon at room temperature has a resistivity near $ 10^{10} \ \Omega\cdot cm $, which doping drives down to between $ 10^{-2} $ and $ 1 \ \Omega\cdot cm $.

## Devices and process scaling

A p-n junction forms a diode and the MOSFET forms inverters and logic gates; combined they give the CMOS process. Scaling has taken transistor gate lengths from tens of nanometres down to single digits: TSMC brought 7 nm to volume production in 2018, 5 nm in 2020 and 3 nm in 2022, with 2 nm entering production from 2025. Once the oxide thins below 1 nm, leakage and tunnelling become the limit, so high-permittivity dielectrics and metal gates replace polysilicon.

## Comparing common semiconductor materials

Band gap and carrier mobility decide where each material fits: silicon is the most versatile, gallium arsenide offers several times the mobility and suits high-frequency and fibre-optic devices, while gallium nitride and silicon carbide have wide gaps and high breakdown strength, suiting power devices and blue LEDs.

| Material | Band gap (eV) | Electron mobility (cm²/V·s) | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Silicon | 1.12 | 1,450 | General logic, memory, solar cells |
| Germanium | 0.67 | 3,900 | Infrared detection, solar cells |
| Gallium arsenide | 1.42 | 8,500 | High-frequency communications, fibre optics, emitters |
| Gallium nitride | 3.4 | 1,300 | Blue LEDs, power electronics |
| Silicon carbide | 3.26 | 1,200 | High-voltage and high-temperature power devices |

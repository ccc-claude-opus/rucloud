## Overview

**Genetics** studies how traits pass from parents to offspring and how variation arises and is selected, naturally or artificially. It underpins crop breeding, medical genetics and evolutionary theory alike.

## From Mendel to DNA

Mendel published his pea-crossing results in 1866, proposing the laws of segregation and independent assortment, but they were ignored until three workers rediscovered them independently in 1900. Watson and Crick proposed the double helix in 1953, showing that genetic information is stored as a base sequence and copied by complementary pairing. The Human Genome Project, launched in 1990 and completed in 2003, sequenced roughly $3$ billion base pairs and identified about $20,000$ protein-coding genes.

## Modes of inheritance

How a trait is inherited depends on where its gene sits and how dominance works. A single-gene cross yields a $3 : 1$ phenotypic ratio and a $1 : 2 : 1$ genotypic ratio, and such ratios are described by the Hardy-Weinberg expression $p^2 + 2pq + q^2 = 1$, where $p$ and $q$ are the frequencies of two alleles in the population. When a gene lies on a sex chromosome the phenotypic split between the sexes changes, as in the inheritance of blood groups and colour blindness.

## Variation and mutation rate

Genetic variation arises through mutation and recombination. Each human child carries roughly $60$ to $70$ new mutations, which corresponds to a mutation rate of about $1.2 \times 10^{-8}$ per base pair per generation and keeps appreciable variation in human populations over thousands of years. Recombination is produced by crossing over and independent assortment during meiosis, and it lets natural selection act on several loci at once.

## From inheritance to application

Genetic knowledge has become technique. Marker-assisted selection lets breeders screen for target traits in the field rather than waiting out a full growth cycle, and CRISPR gene editing, announced in 2012, allows precise sequence changes that are now standard in breeding and biomedical research. Epigenetics and genomic imprinting explain why the expression of some genes depends only on the copy inherited from the father or the mother.

| Technique | Principle | Application |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Marker-assisted selection | Screen target genes via molecular markers | Crop breeding |
| Gene editing (CRISPR) | Cut and repair DNA at defined sites | Breeding, biomedical research |
| Sequencing | Read base sequences | Disease diagnosis, relatedness testing |
| Transgenesis | Insert a foreign gene | Insect-resistant crops, gene therapy |

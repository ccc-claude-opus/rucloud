## Overview

**Mathematical analysis** studies functions and their properties rigorously through limits, continuity and calculus, forming the common foundation of the other branches of mathematics. Unlike elementary mathematics, every conclusion is derived step by step from explicit definitions.

## Limits and continuity

A limit is defined rigorously as follows: the statement $\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L$ means that for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there exists $\delta > 0$ such that $0 < |x - a| < \delta$ implies $|f(x) - L| < \varepsilon$ throughout, which is far more precise than saying the values merely get closer. A function is continuous at a point when the limit exists and equals the function value there, and it is differentiable when the difference quotient $\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}$ exists.

## Calculus and series

The derivative $f'(x)$ gives the instantaneous rate of change, an antiderivative $F(x)$ satisfies $F'(x) = f(x)$ and the fundamental theorem of calculus links the two through the identity $\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = F(b) - F(a)$ for every interval. A definite integral is computed as the limit of Riemann sums, while Taylor series expand a function as an infinite power series, for instance $e^x = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n!}$ in its standard form.

## Main branches and applications

Analysis divides by subject, with different tools in each area.

| Branch | Object of study | Core tools |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Real analysis | Functions and series on the reals | The $\varepsilon$ - $\delta$ language of limits |
| Complex analysis | Holomorphic functions and residues | Cauchy's integral theorem |
| Measure theory | Length and integration for general sets | Lebesgue measure |
| Functional analysis | Infinite-dimensional function spaces | Banach and Hilbert spaces |

Analytic methods underpin numerical work in engineering mechanics, fluid dynamics and quantum mechanics, since the single formula $f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}$ carries most of that derivation and gives numerical integration and differential equations a rigorous theoretical basis.

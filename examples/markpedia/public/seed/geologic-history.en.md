## Overview

**Geologic history** reconstructs the $4.54$ billion year evolution of the Earth from rock layers, strata and fossils. Built on radiometric dating and stratigraphic correlation, it divides the vast span of time into a sequence of recognisable geological units.

## Age of the Earth and dating

The Earth formed roughly $4.54$ billion years ago, a figure derived from isotope ratios measured in meteorites and in the oldest terrestrial minerals. The useful radioisotopes differ widely in half-life: carbon-14 decays in only $5730$ years and suits samples up to a few tens of thousands of years old, whereas uranium-238 takes $4.47$ billion years and covers the geological scale.

## The geological time scale

The International Commission on Stratigraphy organises Earth history into the Hadean, Archean, Proterozoic and Phanerozoic, with the Phanerozoic dividing into the Paleozoic, Mesozoic and Cenozoic, and each era subdivided into periods and epochs. Official figures are given in millions of years.

| Eon | Start | Age today | Landmark events |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hadean | $4567$ Ma | $4.567$ billion years | Earth forms; ocean and atmosphere appear |
| Archean | $4031$ Ma | $4.031$ billion years | First continents and earliest life |
| Proterozoic | $2500$ Ma | $2.5$ billion years | Great Oxidation Event, red beds |
| Phanerozoic | $538.8$ Ma | $539$ million years | Animal diversification, plants on land |

## Major events in life history

The thread running through Earth history is the interaction of life and environment. The Great Oxidation Event around $2.4$ billion years ago transformed the atmosphere, the Cambrian explosion $539$ million years ago triggered a rapid rise in animal size and diversity, and the Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction $66$ million years ago ended the reign of the dinosaurs. Ice ages and warm intervals alternated throughout, each shifting sea level and redistributing organisms.

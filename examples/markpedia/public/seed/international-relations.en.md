## Overview

**International relations** studies the distribution of power and the interaction and cooperation among states and other international actors. It is both a discipline and a practice, covering diplomacy, international law, international organisations and global governance.

## Actors and issues

States have long been the principal international actors, but since the twentieth century international organisations, multinational firms, nongovernmental organisations and subnational units have gained substantial importance. Issues have likewise widened from war and security to economics, trade, environment, health, information governance and human rights, making the field highly interdisciplinary. The unit of analysis ranges from a single summit to the architecture of global governance.

## Three theoretical orientations

| Orientation | Core claim | Representative figures |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Realism | Without a central authority, states pursue power and security in an anarchic system | Hobbes, Morgenthau, Waltz |
| Liberalism | Institutions, economic interdependence and restraint among democracies reduce conflict | Locke, Kant, Keohane |
| Constructivism | Identities and norms are shaped by interaction; state interests are not fixed | Wendt, Onuf, Harris |

## Institutions and organisations

International institutions are long-standing rules and procedures that states create and come to expect each other to observe, constraining the range of choices open to them. Key bodies include the United Nations with 193 member states, the World Trade Organization, which reached 166 members in August 2024, the International Monetary Fund with 191 members, and NATO with 32 members after Sweden joined in 2024. By supplying information, lowering transaction costs and creating accountability, institutions underpin much international cooperation.

## Measuring power

Power has no single metric, and economic and military capacity usually receive the most attention. SIPRI data put global military spending at about 2.44 trillion USD in 2023, up roughly 6.8 % on the year, with United States outlays above 900 billion USD, or close to 40 % of the world total. Population size, trade dependence, technological capacity and institutional influence also enter the reckoning, but no single number captures a complex distribution of power.

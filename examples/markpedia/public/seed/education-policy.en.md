## Overview

**Education policy** is the government's approach to the structure, funding, standards and equity goals of an education system. It determines who gets access to which learning opportunities and is usually among the largest categories of public spending.

## Policy instruments and division of responsibility

Education policy splits into demand-side and supply-side instruments. Demand-side tools — compulsory schooling, scholarships, school meals and fee waivers — lower the cost of attending and remove obstacles; supply-side tools cover school establishment, teacher training, curriculum standards, assessment systems and digital learning environments. In most countries the centre sets curricula and quality benchmarks while local authorities run schools and staff, so how that split is drawn determines whether policy actually reaches classrooms.

## System structure and years of compulsory schooling

Typical systems run three or four stages: early childhood, primary, secondary and tertiary education. UNESCO statistics show most countries require about 12 years of compulsory schooling, typically 6 years of primary plus 6 years of secondary. Taiwan's twelve-year basic education has applied since the 2019–2020 school year, extending lower secondary into upper secondary, while the primary entry age fell from 6 to 5 in August 2021, reshaping class sizes and teacher demand.

## Inputs and equity indicators

| Indicator | Reference value | What it shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public education spending as share of GDP | About 5 % on average across OECD | Scale of national investment |
| Spending per student | Differences of more than threefold | Fairness of resource allocation |
| Preschool enrolment | Close to 100 % in developed countries | Long-term effect of early intervention |
| Urban–rural spending gap | Above 20 % in some countries | Equality of opportunity between areas |

## Evaluating results and international comparison

PISA assesses 15-year-olds in reading, mathematics and science every three years, with the 2022 round delayed by the pandemic in most countries. In 2022 the OECD mathematics average was about 472 points and Taiwan's about 483; in science the OECD averaged roughly 485 against Taiwan's 490. Such assessments are widely used to check policy effects, but their bias toward tested achievement draws criticism and requires reading alongside curriculum reform and learning-portfolio data.

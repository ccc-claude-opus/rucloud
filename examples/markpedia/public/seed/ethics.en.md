## Overview

**Ethics** asks what is good and what one ought to do, and studies how a person should act in concrete situations. It is part of moral philosophy and also a practical discipline applied to medicine, law, and the environment.

## Three levels of inquiry

Ethics is usually split into three levels: metaethics asks what goodness is and whether moral judgments state facts or express norms; normative ethics answers what one ought to do; applied ethics brings those principles into fields such as medicine, the environment, and animals. Work at the three levels can reinforce or undercut one another — the distinction between what is and what ought to be shapes how medical resource allocation is argued.

## Three theoretical orientations

Chinese thought offers comparable resources: the Confucians center the moral life on ren and reciprocity, insisting that virtue is practiced in concrete relationships, while the Mohists argue for universal love and against aggression and evaluate actions by their practical effect. Set against the European mainstream, these strands form the basic map of ethics today.

| Orientation | Where the criterion lies | Representative figures |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Utilitarianism (consequentialism) | Overall utility of the consequences | Bentham, An Introduction to the Principles of Morals and Legislation (1789); Mill, Utilitarianism (1863) |
| Deontology (Kantian ethics) | Motive and universalizable maxim | Kant, Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals (1785); the categorical imperative |
| Virtue ethics | Character and practical wisdom | Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics; the doctrine of the mean |

## Application and institutions

The scope of applied ethics is now wide, covering responsibility for artificial intelligence, criteria for allocating organs, fairness in climate change, and corporate sustainability. Not every dispute, however, can be settled by philosophical argument: public health agencies, courts, and international organizations still have to turn ethical principles into operable rules and weigh them when values conflict irreconcilably.

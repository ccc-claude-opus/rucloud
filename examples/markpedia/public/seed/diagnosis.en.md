## Overview

**Diagnosis** is the process by which clinicians identify a disease and gauge its severity from history, symptoms, examination and test results. It underpins every subsequent treatment decision and is a central measure of care quality and safety.

## Symptoms, signs and evidence

A subjective complaint described by the patient is a **symptom**, such as fever or cough; an abnormality the clinician can directly observe or measure is a **sign**, such as a temperature of $38.5$ °C. Because every sign can be tested for presence or absence in a binary way, it can be quantified by sensitivity and specificity. Ruling out impossible causes one by one builds the **differential diagnosis**.

## Statistical performance of a test

Sensitivity is the share of true patients who test positive, and specificity the share of healthy people who test negative. The positive predictive value (PPV) depends on both test performance and **prevalence**: for a group of $10\,000$ people with $1$ % prevalence, $90$ % sensitivity and $95$ % specificity, there are $100$ patients, $90$ positives and $495$ false positives, giving PPV = $90/(90+495) \approx 15$ %. When prevalence is low, even an accurate test produces mostly false positives, so the **pre-test probability** must always be considered.

| Parameter | Definition | Clinical meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sensitivity | Share of patients testing positive | Risk of missed disease |
| Specificity | Share of healthy people testing negative | Risk of false alarms |
| Positive predictive value | Share of positives that are truly diseased | Varies markedly with prevalence |
| Negative predictive value | Share of negatives that are truly disease-free | Ability to rule disease out |

## Diagnostic pathways and the gold standard

Clinical diagnosis advances along a path of history, physical examination, initial laboratory testing, imaging or invasive testing, and finally pathological confirmation. The **gold standard** is the most accurate reference for a disease even when it is costly or unsafe, such as histopathology for malignancy. Diagnostic criteria often cite explicit thresholds: diabetes is diagnosed by fasting glucose $\geq 126$ mg/dL or HbA1c $\geq 6.5$ %, while hypertension uses clinic blood pressure $\geq 140/90$ mmHg.

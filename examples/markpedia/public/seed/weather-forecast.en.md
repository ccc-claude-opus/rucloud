## Overview

**Weather forecasting** converts the current state of the atmosphere into a projection of the future. It is essentially an initial-value problem: errors accumulate with time, so forecasts have a horizon and necessarily carry a probabilistic character.

## Observation and assimilation

The first step in numerical prediction is merging surface, radiosonde, satellite and radar observations into a consistent initial field. This data assimilation adjusts observations to satisfy the model's equations; modern schemes such as four-dimensional variational assimilation optimise the evolution as well as the state.

## Numerical models

Global forecast models now resolve the horizontal at a few kilometres across more than a hundred vertical layers, computing the full flow field in reasonable time. As supercomputing capacity grew, ensemble forecasting became practical: running many slightly perturbed initial conditions yields probabilities rather than a single line.

## Measuring skill

| Metric | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| RMSE | Root-mean-square difference from observations; lower is better |
| Brier score | Calibration score for probability forecasts; lower is better |
| Hit rate | Fraction of actual weather events correctly forecast |

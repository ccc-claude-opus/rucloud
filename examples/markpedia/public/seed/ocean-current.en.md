## Overview

An **ocean current** is a large-scale directed flow of seawater driven by wind, the Coriolis effect and density differences. By redistributing heat and salt between low and high latitudes, currents shape the global climate and the productivity of the world's fisheries.

## What drives currents

Surface currents are driven mainly by wind stress, and the Coriolis parameter $f = 2\Omega \sin \varphi$ grows with latitude, so wind-driven flow turns right in the Northern Hemisphere and left in the Southern. This produces the subtropical gyres. Deep currents are driven instead by density contrasts in temperature and salinity: dense water sinking near the North Atlantic and Antarctica feeds the global thermohaline circulation, exchanging surface and deep water over millennia.

## Major currents and their transport

Transport varies enormously among currents, with $1$ Sv equal to one million cubic metres per second. The North Atlantic Drift carries heat into northwestern Europe and is the direct reason for ice-free ports at high latitude.

| Current | Location and type | Transport | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Antarctic Circumpolar | Circumnavigates $40$–$60$ °S, cold | $130$–$150$ Sv | Largest current in the world |
| Kuroshio | North past Japan, warm | about $45$ Sv | Warms and moistens the coast |
| Gulf Stream | North off the US east coast, warm | about $30$ Sv | Feeds the North Atlantic Drift |
| Peru (Humboldt) | North along western South America, cold | Upwelling zone | Supports the Peruvian fishery |

## Climatic and ecological effects

Heat carried by currents is measured in petawatts, and the global oceanic transport is of order $1$ PW, enough to reshape the climate of adjacent continents. Upwelling brings nutrient-rich deep water to the surface and supports the major fisheries of Peru, Chile and Namibia, and the year-to-year swings of those stocks track current anomalies directly, such as temperature changes off southern California.

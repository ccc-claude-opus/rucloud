## Overview

**Telecommunications** is the set of technologies and systems that carry information over distance as electrical, optical or radio signals across wired and wireless media. Progress has come from three directions — wider bandwidth, more efficient coding and optical fibre transmission — taking mobile data rates from tens of kbit/s in the 2G era to several Gbit/s with 5G.

## History and the information-theoretic basis

Any periodic signal can be decomposed into a sum of sinusoids of different frequencies by Fourier's theorem, and this is the mathematical precondition for frequency division multiplexing and modulation; Fourier stated it fully in 1822. Hertz confirmed the existence of electromagnetic waves experimentally in 1887, Marconi completed a wireless telegraph demonstration in 1895, and Bell's 1876 telephone patent opened the era of wired voice. Claude Shannon founded information theory in 1948, relating bandwidth $ B $, signal-to-noise ratio $ S/N $ and maximum rate $ C = B \log_2(1 + S/N) $, the standard against which modern systems are designed.

## Transmission media and frequency bands

Optical fibre carries data as light pulses. Single-mode fibre has a core of about 9 µm and works at 1310 nm or 1550 nm, where attenuation reaches only about 0.2 dB/km, allowing tens of terabits per second over tens of kilometres without regeneration; multimode fibre has a 50 or 62.5 µm core at 850 nm with attenuation of 2 to 3 dB/km for short in-building links. On the radio side, 4G LTE offers up to 20 MHz of bandwidth and roughly 1 Gbit/s peak download, while 5G NR reaches 10 Gbit/s in the sub-6 GHz band commonly used at 3.5 GHz and up to 20 Gbit/s at millimetre-wave bands such as 26 GHz. A geostationary orbit sits about 35,786 km away, giving roughly 120 ms one-way and 240 ms round-trip propagation delay.

## Comparing mobile generations

Successive mobile generations differ clearly in bandwidth, peak rate and intended use, and the overall direction has been from voice toward low latency and massive machine-to-machine connectivity.

| Generation | Representative technology | Typical bandwidth | Peak data rate | Main scenario |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2G | GSM, GPRS | 200 kHz | Tens of kbit/s | Digital voice and SMS |
| 3G | WCDMA, HSPA+ | 5 – 20 MHz | A few Mbit/s | Mobile internet adoption |
| 4G | LTE, LTE-Advanced | 20 – 100 MHz | Around 1 Gbit/s | Video streaming |
| 5G | NR FR1, NR FR2 | 100 – 400 MHz | 10 – 20 Gbit/s | IoT, AR/VR, low-latency control |

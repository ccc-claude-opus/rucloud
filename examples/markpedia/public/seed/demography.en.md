## Overview

**Demography** is the study of the size, structure and change of populations. It describes and explains fertility, mortality and migration statistically, and underpins public policy, population economics and historical research alike.

## Subjects and basic measures

Core measures include total population, the crude birth and death rates, and the total fertility rate. The crude birth rate runs near 16 to 17 per thousand worldwide in recent years; the total fertility rate is the average number of children a woman bears over her reproductive life, and below roughly 2.1 it falls under replacement level. Population change combines natural increase with net migration, and both shift the age structure.

## Demographic transition

The transition describes stages developing countries pass through as they industrialise: | Stage | Birth rate | Death rate | Population trend |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First | High | High | Slow growth, short lives |
| Second | High | Falling fast | Growth accelerates |
| Third | Falling | Low | Growth moderates |
| Fourth | Low | Low | Stable or negative growth |
| Fifth | Below replacement | Low | Ageing and contraction |

## Computing growth rates

The annual growth rate approximates to $r = b - d + m$ where $b$ is the crude birth rate, $d$ the crude death rate and $m$ the net migration rate, all per thousand. Doubling time follows the rule of 70 as $T \approx \frac{70}{r}$ where $r$ is the growth rate in percent. A rate of 2 % means a population doubles in about 35 years.

## The global picture today

The UN World Population Prospects 2024 revision puts the world population at about 8.2 billion in 2024, notes that growth peaked around 2020, and expects negative growth in the mid-2080s at a peak of some 10.3 billion. Regional differences matter more: most African countries remain in the second stage of the transition, with doubling times under 30 years, while East Asia and Europe have entered the fifth stage and begun to contract.

## Overview

A **telescope** is an optical instrument that collects light from distant celestial objects and concentrates it into an image. It makes no light of its own, so the larger the aperture, the more light is gathered and the fainter the objects that become visible.

## Two designs

Refracting telescopes bend light through lenses; they are compact and give stable images over a narrow field. Reflecting telescopes use mirrors, work efficiently in the infrared, and dominate large observatories. The first portable telescope appeared in 1608, and Galileo used one to observe the moons of Jupiter, opening a new era in astronomy.

## Resolution versus magnification

Resolution is set by aperture and wavelength: a larger aperture resolves finer detail, and shorter wavelengths (visible light is far shorter than radio) do better. Magnification only enlarges the image — it adds no detail, and pushing past the instrument's resolving power simply blurs the picture.

## Common types and uses

| Type | Character | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Refractor | Sharp, no collimation needed | Observation and imaging |
| Reflector | Apertures scale cheaply | Large professional observatories |
| Schmidt-Cassegrain | Compact and portable | Planetary and lunar imaging |
| Solar telescope | Uses filters to protect the eye | Observing prominences and eclipses |

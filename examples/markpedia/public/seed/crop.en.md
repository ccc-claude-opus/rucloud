## Overview

A **crop** is a plant or cultured organism deliberately grown for human use, forming the base of global food, feed and industrial raw materials. The output and distribution of cereals, oilseeds, vegetables and fruits directly shape food security and agricultural economics.

## Major groups and global scale

Crops are grouped by use into food crops (rice, wheat, maize, legumes), cash crops (cotton, sugarcane, oilseeds, tea) and horticultural crops (vegetables, fruits, ornamentals). World cereal output reaches about $3.2$ billion tonnes a year, and maize, wheat and rice together supply more than four fifths of it. Oilseed production is led by soybean, palm and rapeseed, with soybean alone approaching $400$ million tonnes annually.

## Breeding and yield gains

Yield growth has come mainly from breeding plus agronomy. Semi-dwarf genes such as Rht-B1b and Rht-D1b in wheat were adopted widely after the 1960s, improving lodging resistance in wheat and rice; with intensive fertilization and irrigation, average world cereal yield rose from about $1.4$ t/ha in 1961 to roughly $4.0$ t/ha around 2020, close to three times the former level. Over the same period nitrogen fertilizer use climbed from about $8$ million tonnes to $120$ million tonnes of nitrogen, making it one of the main drivers of that yield rise.

## Biotechnology and sustainable production

Insect-resistant crops such as Bt maize and Bt cotton covered more than $70$ million hectares worldwide in 2019, cutting the number of insecticide applications and the associated environmental load. Production is nevertheless constrained by drought, heat and soil degradation, and roughly one third of the world's arable land shows some degree of degradation. Water-saving irrigation, cover crops and rotation have therefore become mainstream, and yield gains are increasingly measured as output per unit of water and nutrient rather than tonnes per hectare alone.

## Overview

**Drug action** is the mechanism by which a drug binds a target and alters physiological function. It splits into **pharmacodynamics**, which describes the relation between concentration and effect, and **pharmacokinetics**, which describes what the body does to the drug.

## Binding to the target

Most drugs act by binding a **target** molecule such as a receptor, an enzyme, an ion channel or nucleic acid, and most such binding is reversible. Receptor occupancy can be written $A_{occ} = \frac{[A]}{[A] + K_d}$, where a smaller $K_d$ means higher affinity. Many drugs are **selective** for receptor subtypes, which is why one class of drug produces different effects in different tissues.

## Pharmacodynamic models

Pharmacodynamics describes how concentration translates into effect, most often with the **$E_{max}$ model** $E = \frac{E_{max} \cdot C}{EC_{50} + C}$. The $EC_{50}$ is the concentration producing half the maximum effect, so a smaller value means less drug is needed for the same response. **Potency** compares the concentrations needed for equal effects, while **efficacy** compares the maximum achievable effect, so a potent drug need not be an efficacious one. A **partial agonist** can reduce net signalling once occupancy passes about $50$ %.

## Antagonism and safety margins

Antagonists are either **competitive**, competing for the same binding site and surmountable by raising the agonist concentration, or **non-competitive**, binding elsewhere and not surmountable. The two can be separated by Schild analysis, plotting the logarithm of receptor occupancy against antagonist concentration to obtain $pA_2$. Safety margin is often expressed as the therapeutic index $TI = \frac{TD_{50}}{ED_{50}}$, and the larger it is the wider the therapeutic window.

| Parameter | Meaning | Clinical reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $EC_{50}$ | Half-maximal effective concentration | Potency and starting dose |
| $E_{max}$ | Maximum effect | Ceiling on achievable response |
| $pA_2$ | Index of antagonist affinity | Comparing antagonistic potency |
| Therapeutic index | $TD_{50}/ED_{50}$ | Width of the safety margin |

## Overview

**Medication safety** comprises the practices that prevent misuse, overdose and harmful interactions: managing high-risk medicines, reviewing polypharmacy, and building error defences into every step from prescribing to monitoring.

## Sources of risk

Medication safety covers **adverse drug reactions**, the expected pharmacological effects that cause harm, and **medication errors**, which occur at prescribing, dispensing, administration or monitoring. High-risk situations include advanced age, impaired liver or kidney function, attending several specialists, complex regimens and high-alert drugs such as anticoagulants, insulin and opioids. In $2017$ the World Health Organization launched the Medication Without Harm initiative and in $2019$ published a list of $20$ classes of high-risk medicines, urging countries to prioritise reducing the harm they cause.

## Polypharmacy and interactions

Taking several medicines at once raises the risk of interactions and of falls, and a person over 65 on more than $5$ drugs is a common case of **polypharmacy**. The Beers criteria and the STOPP criteria list the combinations to avoid. Classic examples include warfarin with non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs causing bleeding, an ACE inhibitor with a potassium-sparing diuretic causing hyperkalaemia, and grapefruit juice inhibiting **CYP3A4** to raise the blood level of certain statins.

## Principles of safe use

The practical rules are the **five rights** — right patient, right drug, right dose, right route and right time — together with reading the prescription three times before administration. The FDA's former letter grades for pregnancy, from A to X, were replaced in $2015$ with narrative descriptions stressing the weight of evidence. Outside hospital, patients are advised to bring a complete medication list to every visit, covering prescriptions, over-the-counter products, herbal remedies and supplements.

| Risk situation | Common cause | Preventive practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dose error | Misreading conversion or dose label | Double-check, label with units |
| Drug interaction | Mixing prescribed and self-started medicines | Update the list at every visit |
| Failure to adjust for renal function | Ignoring creatinine clearance | Monitor blood tests regularly |
| Therapeutic duplication | Same ingredient under different names | Confirm by generic name |

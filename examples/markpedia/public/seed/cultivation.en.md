## Overview

**Cultivation** is the full set of field practices that prepares land for sowing and keeps it productive, covering tillage, planting, fertilization, irrigation and crop management. These practices determine yield, cost and long-term soil health.

## Field operations and planting

A typical cycle runs through tillage, planting, crop management and harvest. Tillage inverts and breaks soil, usually to a depth of $15$ to $20$ cm, which improves aeration but also speeds up organic matter breakdown. Seeding rates vary by crop and row spacing: drilled rice uses about $30$ to $40$ kg of seed per hectare, while wheat is sown at roughly $120$ to $200$ kg/ha.

| Operation | Purpose | Common methods |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tillage | Loosen, level and mix soil | Ploughing, harrowing, levelling |
| Planting | Establish plant density | Drilling, spot sowing, transplanting |
| Fertilizing | Supply nutrients | Basal, top dressing, fertigation |
| Irrigation | Supply water | Flood, furrow, sprinkler, drip |
| Pest management | Limit damage | Rotation, biological control, IPM |

## Irrigation and water management

Rice is among the most water-demanding crops, and conventional flooded irrigation consumes roughly $2,000$ to $3,000$ litres of water for every kilogram of paddy produced, with seepage and evaporation adding to the loss. Delivery efficiency differs sharply by method: drip irrigation exceeds $90$ %, sprinkler systems reach about $70$ %, and flooding often stays below $50$ %. Techniques such as alternate wetting and drying cut water use by $15$ % to $30$ % without yield loss while also suppressing methane formation in the paddy.

## Soil and nutrient management

Soil management determines whether fertility can be sustained. The world applies about $110$ million tonnes of nitrogen fertilizer each year, roughly a third of which eventually reaches animals as feed. Leguminous green manures fix $80$ to $200$ kg of nitrogen per hectare while adding organic carbon and biological diversity. No-till combined with cover crops sharply reduces wind and water erosion, with trials reporting $30$ % to $80$ % less topsoil loss, though an initial adjustment period of higher weed pressure often accompanies the switch.

## Mechanization and precision farming

Mechanization is the core measure of agricultural labour productivity, and tractors, combine harvesters and rice reapers have raised the area one worker can manage from a few hectares to several tens of hectares. Around $3.6$ million tractors and $1.7$ million combine harvesters are in operation worldwide, with distribution highly uneven towards developing regions. Global positioning, soil sensors and machine vision have more recently enabled precision agriculture, in which fertilizer is applied by soil management zone; field trials report input savings of $10$ % to $20$ %.

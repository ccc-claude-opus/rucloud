## Overview

A **cell** is the basic unit of life: a membrane-bound compartment carrying genetic material and metabolism that can replicate and organize into tissues. From single-celled bacteria to the human body, every living form is built from cells.

## Definition and the cell theory

The cell theory was set out by Schleiden and Schwann between 1838 and 1839, holding that all animals and plants are composed of cells and that the cell is the basic unit of life; Virchow added in 1855 that every cell arises from a pre-existing cell. Hooke examined cork with a compound microscope in 1665 and drew the first honeycomb of "cells", and Brown identified the nucleus in orchid cells in 1831, establishing the basic architecture of eukaryotic cells.

## Prokaryotes and eukaryotes

Cells divide into prokaryotic and eukaryotic types according to whether they possess a nuclear envelope and membrane-bound organelles. Bacteria and archaea are prokaryotic, usually $1$ to $10$ micrometres across, with DNA concentrated in a nucleoid region. Animals, plants, fungi and protists are eukaryotic, usually $10$ to $100$ micrometres across, with a nucleus, mitochondria, endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi apparatus; plant cells add a wall, chloroplasts and a large central vacuole.

| Feature | Prokaryotic cell | Eukaryotic cell |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nucleus | Absent, DNA in nucleoid | Enclosed by nuclear envelope |
| DNA form | Usually circular, single strand | Linear, multiple strands with proteins |
| Membrane organelles | Absent | Several types present |
| Typical size | 1–10 µm | 10–100 µm |
| Examples | Bacteria, archaea | Animals, plants, fungi |

## Scale and numbers

Cell size is constrained by the surface-area to volume ratio: scaling a cell by a factor $k$ multiplies surface area by $k^2$ but volume by $k^3$, so large organisms need many specialized cells to keep exchange rates adequate. The human body contains about $3.7 \times 10^{13}$ cells, of which some $86$ billion are neurons, while the estimated number of bacteria on Earth reaches $3.8 \times 10^{30}$, the majority of them living in soil and marine sediment.

## Division, transport and energy

Cells proliferate through mitosis under a controlled cycle, and human cells cultured in vitro usually divide $40$ to $50$ times before entering senescence, a limit known as the Hayflick limit. Most energy is generated in mitochondria, each of which carries hundreds to thousands of copies of its own DNA; human mitochondrial DNA is $16,569$ base pairs long and the organelle depends on products encoded by nuclear genes, a relationship that testifies to an ancient endosymbiotic origin of eukaryotic cells.

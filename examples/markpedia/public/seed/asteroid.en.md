## Overview

An **asteroid** is a small rocky or metallic body orbiting the Sun, ranging from a few metres to nearly a thousand kilometres. Most lie in the main belt between Mars and Jupiter, while near-Earth asteroids cross Earth's own orbit.

## Why the name

The Greek planetes means wanderer. After Uranus and Neptune were found in the early nineteenth century, astronomers suspected an undiscovered planet between Mars and Jupiter and searched for it under planetary names, finding instead a range of much smaller bodies — hence asteroid, the lesser planet.

## Classes and structure

By appearance and spectrum they fall into three broad types: C-types (carbonaceous, rich in carbon and water), S-types (silicate, mostly metal and silicate rock) and M-types (metallic). Ceres, the largest known asteroid, spans about 940 km — wide enough for gravity to pull it nearly spherical.

## Impacts and resources

Asteroid impacts are recorded in craters and rock layers on Earth. Probe missions such as Hayabusa and China's Chang'e 2 T-type mission examine composition and structure; the palladium and nickel content of near-Earth asteroids makes them candidates for future space mining.

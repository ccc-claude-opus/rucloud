## Overview

A **rocket** is a vehicle propelled by the thrust of hot, high-pressure exhaust gas produced by combustion. It carries its own propellant and oxidiser, so it works in vacuum — at the cost of a low thrust-to-weight ratio and expensive launches.

## Basic principle

Thrust comes from conservation of momentum: $F = \dot{m} v_e$, where $\dot{m}$ is the mass flow rate and $v_e$ the exhaust velocity. Two levers raise thrust — a higher burn rate, or a more energetic propellant. Hydrogen–oxygen reaches about $4.5$ km/s of exhaust velocity against $3.4$ km/s for kerosene.

## Multistage design

A single stage cannot escape Earth's gravity on its own, so launch vehicles stage: each spent stage is discarded, shedding mass. The Saturn V's three stages accumulated enough velocity increment to reach the Moon precisely this way.

## Key orbital numbers

| Parameter | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Specific impulse $I_{sp}$ | Effective impulse per kilogram of propellant; higher is more efficient |
| Δv | Velocity increment, setting what orbit or altitude a mission can reach |
| Mass ratio | Structural and propellant ratios, determining how many stages are needed |

## Overview

**Astrometry** is the branch of astronomy that measures the positions, distances and motions of celestial objects. Its precision sets the quality of star catalogues, the reliability of dynamical models of the solar system, and it makes the discovery and orbital confirmation of exoplanets possible.

## The precision of angular measurement

Ground-based optical work is limited by atmospheric turbulence and parallax. Moving telescopes into space improved precision by orders of magnitude. The Gaia survey has measured positions and parallaxes for more than a billion stars, enough to reconstruct the kinematic structure of the Milky Way.

## The parallax distance method

As the Earth orbits the Sun, the line of sight to a distant object shifts periodically. The parallax angle $p$ and the distance $d$ are related by $d = 1 / p$ (in parsecs, with $p$ in arcseconds). Because the angle is minuscule, a very precise reference catalogue is needed for comparison.

## Results

- Reconstructing the age and kinematics of the Galaxy from Gaia and Hipparcos parallaxes.
- Tracking exoplanet transits and radial velocities with precision optical and infrared measurements.
- Discovering Proxima Centauri in 1868 through the periodic aberration of its light.

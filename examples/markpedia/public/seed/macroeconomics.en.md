## Overview

**Macroeconomics** studies the economy as a whole, focusing on aggregate output, employment and prices. It deals with inflation, business cycles, unemployment and the policies through which governments and central banks respond.

## The expenditure identity

On the expenditure side, gross domestic product equals consumption $ C $ plus investment $ I $ plus government purchases $ G $ plus net exports $ (X - M) $: $ Y = C + I + G + (X - M) $. This is an accounting identity, but writing the components as functions turns it into testable propositions; in the consumption function $ C = a + bY $ the marginal propensity to consume $ b $ lies between 0 and 1.

## Multipliers and the business cycle

A unit of government spending is multiplied as it is respent, giving a multiplier $ k = \frac{1}{1 - b} $ on output. With $ b = 0.8 $ the multiplier is 5, so 100 US dollars of final demand may support roughly 500 US dollars of output; in practice import leakages and a higher saving rate make it smaller. The business cycle is the fluctuation of output around its long-run trend; US real GDP fell about 3.4 % in 2020, the deepest annual contraction since the war.

## Prices, employment and policy tools

| Concept | How it is measured | Policy response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inflation | Year-on-year change in the consumer price index | Tighten monetary or fiscal policy |
| Unemployment | Share of the labour force actively seeking work | Expand aggregate demand and retrain workers |
| Real interest rate | Nominal rate minus inflation | Changes incentives to invest and save |
| Money supply | Currency in circulation plus bank deposits | Central banks adjust it through open market operations |

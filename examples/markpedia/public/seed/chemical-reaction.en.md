## Overview

**A chemical reaction** is the process in which reactants are recombined into products, breaking old chemical bonds and forming new ones. The number and kinds of atoms are conserved across the reaction, even though the form of energy and the states of matter change markedly.

## Equations and the mole

Chemical equations obey conservation of mass, so burning hydrogen is written $2H_2 + O_2 \rightarrow 2H_2O$ with the smallest whole-number coefficients. Amount of substance is measured in moles, following the relation $n = m / M$ where $M$ is the molar mass, and Avogadro's constant is $N_A = 6.022 \times 10^{23}$ mol⁻¹. Thus $18$ g of water, with $M \approx 18$ g/mol, contains about $6.022 \times 10^{23}$ molecules.

## Reaction rate and activation energy

Rates are usually expressed as $v = k[A]^m[B]^n$ with the orders $m$ and $n$ determined experimentally, while the effect of temperature on the rate constant follows the Arrhenius equation:

$$k = A e^{-E_a / (RT)}$$

where $A$ is the pre-exponential factor, $E_a$ the activation energy, $R \approx 8.314$ J/(mol·K) and $T$ the absolute temperature. Empirically, raising the temperature by $10$ °C speeds a reaction up by a factor of two to three. A catalyst offers an alternative pathway of lower activation energy, changing the rate but not the equilibrium constant or the overall reaction enthalpy.

## Equilibrium and Le Chatelier's principle

A reversible reaction reaches chemical equilibrium when forward and reverse rates are equal. The equilibrium constant $K_c$ is fixed at a given temperature and depends only on that temperature. The effects of changing conditions are as follows.

| Condition | Effect on rate | Effect on equilibrium |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Raise temperature | Reactions speed up | Equilibrium shifts endothermically |
| Add a catalyst | Both directions speed up | Equilibrium constant unchanged |
| Increase reactant concentration | Rate increases | Equilibrium shifts to products |
| Raise pressure (gases) | Rate increases | Equilibrium shifts to fewer gas moles |

This is Le Chatelier's principle, which predicts how a system responds to a change in conditions.

## Redox reactions and energy change

Oxidation–reduction reactions are identified by changes in oxidation number: when carbon burns to $CO_2$ its oxidation number rises from $0$ to $+4$ in the process. Exothermic reactions raise the temperature of the surroundings while endothermic ones cool it, and in both cases energy is conserved, since the reaction enthalpy equals the total bond energy of the products minus that of the reactants.

## Overview

**Personal finance** is the planning of income, spending, saving and investing by individuals or households. It turns life goals into workable cash-flow arrangements and is the entry point for most people's financial planning.

## Cash flow and emergency reserves

The first step in personal finance is to establish monthly disposable cash flow, separating fixed necessities from flexible spending, and to hold an emergency reserve against the unexpected. A common rule of thumb covers 3 to 6 months of basic living costs; households with unstable income or a single source of earnings usually need more, sometimes a 12-month cushion.

## Compounding and time

Principal and accumulated returns work together, giving $ A = P(1 + r)^n $ where $ P $ is principal, $ r $ the periodic rate and $ n $ the number of periods. The lower the rate, the longer doubling takes; the common shortcut divides 72 by the rate in percentage points, so 5 % needs about 14.4 years to double while 2 % needs roughly 36.

## Asset allocation and risk management

| Item | Instruments | Main considerations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cash and deposits | Current accounts, time deposits | Trade-off between liquidity and yield |
| Fixed income | Government and corporate bonds, bond funds | Interest-rate and credit risk |
| Growth assets | Shares, equity funds, ETFs | Long-run growth with higher volatility |
| Risk transfer | Insurance, annuities | Shifts tail risks that cannot be absorbed |
| Tax arrangements | Retirement accounts, preferential rates | Deferring tax to compound returns longer |

## Overview

A **financial statement** is a structured periodic report describing a company's financial position, operating results and cash flows. Investors, creditors and regulators use it to assess solvency and performance.

## Composition of the main statements

The balance sheet is a static statement of assets, liabilities and equity at a single date, balancing through the identity $ A = L + E $. The income statement covers a period of revenues and expenses and yields net income. The cash flow statement separates operating, investing and financing flows, showing whether reported profit actually turned into cash. A statement of changes in equity links opening and closing balances.

## Accrual basis and accounting standards

Financial statements apply accrual accounting: revenue and expense are recorded when transactions occur, not when cash moves. Leases and supplier financing therefore leave traces on the balance sheet without affecting current cash flow. In Taiwan, listed issuers have applied IFRSs in full since the first quarter of 2023, and IFRSs for SMEs has applied to smaller entities since 1 January 2024.

## Common analysis ratios

| Ratio | Formula | What it shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gross margin | $ (Revenue - COGS) / Revenue $ | Pricing power and cost control |
| Net margin | $ Net\ income / Revenue $ | Profit kept from each unit of revenue |
| Return on equity | $ Net\ income / Average\ equity $ | Efficiency of shareholders' capital |
| Return on assets | $ Net\ income / Average\ total\ assets $ | Efficiency of asset use |
| Current ratio | $ Current\ assets / Current\ liabilities $ | Short-term solvency, traditionally around 2 |

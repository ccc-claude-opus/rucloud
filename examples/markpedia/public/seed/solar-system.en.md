## Overview

The **Solar System** is the Sun together with the planets, moons, minor bodies and debris held by its gravity, formed from the collapse of a nebula about 4.6 billion years ago. It is the most direct laboratory in astrophysics, offering a complete sample from rocky worlds to gas giants.

## Members and zones

The main members are eight planets, split into the rocky inner planets (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars) and the giant outer planets (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune). Dwarf planets such as Pluto and Ceres form a separate class, while the Kuiper belt and scattered disc supply most small bodies.

## Scale and time

Light takes about 8 minutes 20 seconds to travel from the Sun to the Earth, and the Sun takes roughly 230 million years to orbit the Galaxy. More than 99.8% of the system's mass sits in the Sun, which is why planetary orbits are close to circular and share a plane.

## How it formed

As the primordial solar nebula collapsed, its centre ignited into a protostar while dust grains in the disc collided and stuck together, growing into planetesimals, then through runaway growth into planetary embryos, and finally into today's configuration through impacts and accretion.

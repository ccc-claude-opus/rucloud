## Overview

**Infrastructure** comprises the transport, energy, water and communications facilities and systems that keep a society functioning, usually provided by governments or regulated public utilities. The scale of investment shapes economic efficiency, urban competitiveness and resilience to disasters, and it is the hardest category of public spending to evaluate over short horizons.

## Definition, scope and financing

Infrastructure divides into hard assets such as roads, pipelines, dams and power stations, and soft assets such as information systems and institutions; the two must come together to deliver benefits. Financing takes the form of government budgets, public enterprise funds, bond issues and public-private partnerships, in which a private party builds an asset and recovers its cost from user fees or government payments. Because these assets are long-lived, financial appraisal is spread over 30 to 100 years.

## Scale and demand drivers

Urbanisation is the largest source of infrastructure demand. The United Nations estimates that the share of the global population living in cities will rise from 50 % in 2007 to 68 % by 2050, with electricity, water and transport needs growing in step. The International Energy Agency reports global generation of about 29,900 TWh in 2023, with renewables close to 30 %, pushing grid expansion and storage to the centre of investment. In developed regions, spending on ageing pipes and bridges often exceeds spending on new construction.

## Performance indicators by sector

Each field of infrastructure has its own representative performance indicators, used to check service levels and investment returns. The chosen indicators must correspond to the quality users actually perceive, otherwise measurement becomes a formality. The table below lists common measures by field.

| Field | Representative assets | Common indicators |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transport | Roads, railways, ports, airports | Accessibility per capita, travel time, peak saturation |
| Energy | Power stations, grids, storage | Reliability of supply, peak load, cost per kWh |
| Water | Reservoirs, distribution mains, sewers, levees | Water security, leakage rate, flood exposure |
| Communications | Backbone networks, data centres | Bandwidth, end-to-end latency, coverage |
| Digital | Information platforms and system integration | System availability, transaction processing time |

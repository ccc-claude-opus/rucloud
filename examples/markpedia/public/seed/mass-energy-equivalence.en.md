## Overview

**Mass–energy equivalence** holds that mass and energy are two aspects of the same physical quantity and can be converted into one another. In 1905 Einstein expressed this in the form $E = mc^2$ which shows that mass is itself stored energy.

## The formula and its scale

The mass–energy relation takes the form $E = mc^2$ and is written in nuclear reactions as $E = \Delta m c^2$ where $\Delta m$ is the mass defect of the system, a change of symbol rather than of meaning. Because $c \approx 2.998 \times 10^8$ m/s, one kilogram corresponds to roughly $9 \times 10^{16}$ J, equivalent to about 21 million tonnes of TNT, since one tonne of TNT releases about $4.184 \times 10^9$ J.

## Chemical versus nuclear reactions

Mass–energy conversion also holds in chemistry, but the effect is minute: burning $1$ kg of coal releases about $3 \times 10^7$ J, a mass defect of only some $3 \times 10^{-10}$ kg, far below the resolution of ordinary balances. Nuclear mass defects are far larger, with $1$ kg of uranium-235 releasing about $8 \times 10^{13}$ J, a defect near $9 \times 10^{-4}$ kg.

| Process | Mass defect | Equivalent energy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Burning $1$ kg of coal | about $3 \times 10^{-10}$ kg | about $3 \times 10^7$ J |
| Fission of $1$ kg of uranium-235 | about $9 \times 10^{-4}$ kg | about $8 \times 10^{13}$ J |
| Formation of an iron-56 nucleus | about $8.8$ MeV binding energy per nucleus | Binding energy |

## Astronomical scale and experimental tests

The Sun consumes about $4 \times 10^9$ kg of mass per second through fusion, of which roughly $0.7$ % leaves as photon radiation and a further significant share is carried away by neutrinos; this is why it radiates about $3.8 \times 10^{26}$ W. Experiments support the equivalence directly: accelerators measure rest-mass differences converting into kinetic energy, atomic-clock time dilation experiments confirm it, and the observed lifetimes of cosmic-ray muons match the prediction.

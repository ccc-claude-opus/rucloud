## Overview

**Engineering material** is a structural or functional material selected for the mechanical loads, environmental conditions and manufacturing route of the component, falling into metals, polymers, ceramics and composites. Selection is a trade-off among strength, stiffness, toughness, weight, cost and service life, and the differences between material families often span several orders of magnitude.

## Principal property metrics

Stiffness is governed by the Young's modulus $ E $, a measure of resistance to elastic deformation; divided by density it yields specific stiffness, the decisive criterion for aerospace structures. Strength is quoted as yield strength $ \sigma_y $ or ultimate tensile strength $ \sigma_u $, while toughness is captured by fracture toughness $ K_{IC} $ or Charpy impact energy, describing resistance to crack growth. Thermal and electronic uses also require thermal conductivity and dielectric behaviour.

## Comparing common engineering materials

The table below gathers figures for several widely used structural materials, making the trade-off between specific strength and stiffness visible: aluminium has roughly one third the density of steel, and carbon fibre composites combine low density with very high tensile strength. At equal strength, the lighter material reduces both the supporting structure needed and the cost of transport.

| Material | Density (g/cm³) | Tensile strength (MPa) | Young's modulus (GPa) | Thermal conductivity (W/m·K) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Structural steel S355 | 7.85 | 470 – 630 | 210 | 50 |
| Aluminium alloy 6061-T6 | 2.70 | 310 | 69 | 167 |
| Titanium alloy Ti-6Al-4V | 4.51 | 900 – 1,100 | 114 | 7 |
| Carbon fibre composite (CFRP) | 1.6 | 1,500 – 2,500 | 70 – 140 | 5 – 20 |
| Glass fibre composite (GFRP) | 1.9 | 200 – 500 | 20 – 35 | 0.3 |
| Alumina ceramic | 3.95 | 200 – 350 | 300 – 380 | 20 – 30 |

## Material choice and process interaction

Material selection cannot be separated from processing: weldability decides whether high-strength low-alloy steel can be used for ship hulls, castability drives the moulding cost of aluminium vehicle parts, and the directional nature of fibres means the in-plane strength of CFRP can exceed its interlaminar peel strength several times over. Environment matters equally. Reinforced concrete reaches 21 to 28 MPa in compression but only 3 to 5 MPa in tension, so steel bars supply the missing tension.

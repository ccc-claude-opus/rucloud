## Overview

An **exoplanet** is a planet orbiting a star other than the Sun. Confirmed for the first time in 1995, the catalogue has grown to several thousand, most of them comparable in mass and radius to Jupiter or Earth. It is now among the most active areas of astronomy.

## How they are found

The transit method measures the dip in brightness as a planet crosses its star, with the period giving the orbital period. The radial velocity method measures the periodic shift in the star's spectral lines. Combined, they yield the planet's mass; when the orbit is nearly edge-on, a transit also measures the radius directly.

## Common classes

| Type | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Hot Jupiter | Very close to the host star, with a mass near Jupiter's |
| Mini-Neptune | Mass between Neptune's and Earth's |
| Super-Earth | Radius 1.5–2 times Earth's, composition unknown |
| Habitable-zone planet | Orbital distance that may allow liquid water |

## The next step

Once confirmed, spectrographs analyse the atmosphere for hydrogen, water, carbon dioxide and methane to infer composition and formation history. With JWST, atmospheric composition has become measurable for a handful of rocky planets.

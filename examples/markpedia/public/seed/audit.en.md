## Overview

**Audit** is an independent examination of financial information and internal controls that raises confidence in the reported figures. It provides assurance between management's responsibility for the accounts and the users who rely on them, and is mandatory for listed companies in most jurisdictions.

## Risk model and audit procedures

The core of an audit is identifying, assessing and responding to the risk of material misstatement, modelled as $ RMM = IR \times CR \times DR $ where $ IR $ is inherent risk, $ CR $ control risk and $ DR $ detection risk. Materiality is set using account balances, relatedness and volatility — often a threshold of 0.5 % to 1 % of revenue — and then drives the extent of testing and sample size.

## Evidence and audit opinions

To obtain reasonable assurance the auditor must gather sufficient appropriate evidence, using inspection, observation, confirmation, recalculation and analytical procedures. Four opinions can be issued: unqualified, qualified, adverse and disclaimer. Only an unqualified opinion states that the statements are fairly presented, and the report also describes key audit matters and the independence of the engagement team.

## Types and functions of audit

| Type | Subject | Common purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Financial statement audit | Annual and interim accounts | Statutory reporting and disclosure |
| Internal control audit | Controls over financial reporting | Preventing fraud and error |
| Tax audit | Tax returns | Basis for assessment and collection |
| Project audit | A specific project or fund | Evaluating value for money |
| Internal audit | Internal processes | Risk control and process improvement |

## Overview

**Construction method** is the site practice that turns design drawings into a finished building, covering temporary works, foundations, structure, formwork, scaffolding, lifting and plant. The chosen methods determine programme, quality, safety and cost, and are the main source of both margin and risk for a contractor.

## Site sequence and management

A typical project runs from drawing review and construction planning through commencement approval, temporary works, foundations, main structure, fit-out and acceptance, with progress tracked by a critical path method (CPM) network. Quality rests on testing and inspection: concrete is checked by slump test for workability and accepted against 28-day compressive strength, while formwork stripping waits for 75 % of design strength on spans up to 8 m and 100 % beyond that.

## Foundations and excavation

Foundation type follows ground bearing capacity, building weight and groundwater conditions. Where the soil is strong enough and loads light, shallow foundations such as pad, strip and raft footings are used, generally within a few metres of excavation. High-rise buildings, bridges and port structures use deep foundations — bored piles, precast piles and caissons — reaching tens of metres, while excavations deeper than about 1.5 m require retaining walls, bracing and wellpoint dewatering.

## Comparing structural systems

The principal systems are reinforced concrete (RC), precast concrete (PC), structural steel (SC) and steel-reinforced concrete (SRC), and selection trades programme against cost, spatial flexibility and fire performance. A single building may also mix systems, using a steel frame for the core and in-situ concrete for the slabs. The table below compares their characteristics.

| System | Suitable scale | Programme | Cost | Main characteristics |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reinforced concrete (RC) | Low to mid-rise | Medium | Lowest | Good fire resistance, high stiffness, hard to demolish |
| Precast concrete (PC) | Repetitive housing | Short | Medium | Factory-made components, uniform quality, demanding joints |
| Structural steel (SC) | Long-span industrial and steel frames | Shortest | High | Light and fast, needs separate fire protection |
| Steel-reinforced concrete (SRC) | High-rise and hybrid demands | Long | High | Flexible space, limited storey height, longer programme |

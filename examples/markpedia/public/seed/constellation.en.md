## Overview

A **constellation** is a fixed region of the sky defined by the International Astronomical Union, each containing a designated set of stars. The boundaries are arbitrary by human decision and never shift with time or the observer's position, which is why constellations have long served calendars and astrology.

## From star patterns to constellations

The Babylonian system already contained twelve zodiacal constellations matching the path of the Sun through the year. Chinese astronomy instead divided the sky into three enclosures and twenty-eight mansions measured along the celestial equator. The International Astronomical Union formally defined 88 constellations in 1922 and fixed their boundaries in 1930.

## Eighty-eight constellations

| Constellation | Chinese name | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ursa Major | 大熊座 | Home of the Big Dipper |
| Orion | 獵戶座 | Easiest winter constellation to identify |
| Scorpius | 天蠍座 | Contains the red giant Antares |
| Crux | 南十字座 | Reference point for southern navigation |

## Brightness and magnitude

Historically brightness ran from first to sixth magnitude, brightest first; each magnitude step corresponds to a factor of about $2.512$, so a sixth-magnitude star delivers one hundredth the light of a first-magnitude one. Astronomers later extended the scale in the negative direction, putting the Sun at about $-26.7$.

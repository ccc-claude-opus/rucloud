## Overview

**Phonology** studies the sound system of a language: which sounds form contrasts, and how those sounds are distributed by rule. It deals with categories and oppositions rather than with the physical motions of articulation.

## Phonemes and allophones

A phoneme is the smallest unit of sound that distinguishes meaning, whereas an allophone is the actual realization of that unit in a given environment. In Mandarin a front vowel after an unaspirated consonant is articulated retroflex, so phonetically it is $[ʐ]$ while phonologically it remains one $[ʅ]$. Standard Mandarin has about $21$ initials and $16$ finals, together with $4$ tones.

## Minimal pairs and the IPA

Traditional phoneme analysis collects minimal pairs: words differing by exactly one phoneme yet carrying different meanings. The International Phonetic Alphabet, founded with the International Phonetic Association in $1886$, is revised continuously; the current version lists $107$ symbols so that scholars worldwide can transcribe speech precisely in a single notation free of the ambiguities of national spelling systems.

| Language | Minimal pair | Distinguishing feature |
| --- | --- | --- |
| English | ship / sheep | Voiceless vs voiced |
| Japanese | か / が | Voiceless vs voiced |
| Thai | ก / ข | Paired contrast rather than isolated phoneme |
| Mandarin | 爸 / 趴 | Aspiration vs unaspiration |

## Prosody and phonological change

Above the level of segments lies prosody: how syllables divide, where stress falls, how tones carry meaning, and what rhythmic patterns hold. Phonological rules describe how phonemes actually change in context, such as the two readings of English theatre in the early twentieth century or the tone sandhi of Mandarin 不. Most languages divide syllables into onset and rhyme, and the permitted combinations form a syllable-type template.

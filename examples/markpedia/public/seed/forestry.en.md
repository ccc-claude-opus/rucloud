## Overview

**Forestry** is the science and industry devoted to growing, managing and harvesting forests for timber and non-timber products, while forest cover also provides carbon sequestration, water regulation and habitat conservation.

## Scope and statistics

Forests are managed for timber, for protection (watershed, soil conservation, windbreak and bank defence) or for economic products such as fruit, oil and medicinal species, and the core techniques are silviculture, harvesting and forest protection. National classifications and reporting differ, so the Forest Resources Assessment (FRA) standardizes global area and growing-stock data on a five-year cycle so that countries can compare results and plan policy.

## Global forest resources

The world holds about $4.06$ billion hectares of forest, roughly $31$ % of the land surface and about $0.5$ ha per person. Between 2010 and 2020 the total forest area grew by an average of some $4$ million hectares a year, driven by expanding plantations, yet the loss of tropical primary forest and peatland continues because of agricultural expansion, fire and road building.

## Silviculture and wood production

Plantation, tending and regeneration keep forests productive, and rotation length varies by species: pine about $25$ to $30$ years, spruce about $60$ to $80$ years, while tropical rainforest is managed with selective cutting and natural regeneration. World wood production is about $5$ billion cubic metres a year and paper and paperboard output exceeds $400$ million tonnes. China has been the largest importer of wood since the 2000s, sourcing mainly from Russia, Indonesia and North America.

## Conservation, carbon and new industries

Forests are the main terrestrial carbon pool, absorbing roughly $6$ billion to $7$ billion tonnes of carbon dioxide a year, and forestry therefore links directly with carbon credit trading, bioenergy and engineered timber products in decarbonization policy. The same sector must also contain damage from pests such as pine wilt nematode and from fire, and it must preserve the traditional territories and rights of indigenous and local communities.

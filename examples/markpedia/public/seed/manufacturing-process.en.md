## Overview

**Manufacturing process** is the collective term for the methods that turn raw material into parts and finished goods, grouped mainly into material removal, forming, joining and heat treatment. The choice of process determines accuracy, unit cost, throughput and the final mechanical properties, which makes it one of the most consequential decisions in mechanical development.

## Classification of processes

Material removal is exemplified by machining, including turning, milling, planing, grinding and drilling; in turning, 30 % to 50 % of the blank volume may be cut away. Forming processes keep the chemical composition unchanged and alter shape instead, divided into casting, forging, rolling and extrusion. Joining covers welding, bolting and adhesive bonding, while heat treatment adjusts the microstructure through heating and cooling — quenching for hardness, tempering to relieve stress, carburising to harden a surface layer.

## Accuracy and digital manufacturing

Computer numerical control machining holds contour accuracy to about $ \pm 0.01 $ mm, with coordinate measuring machines verifying deviations down to roughly 1 to 2 µm. Laser cutting is non-contact and tool-free, with a narrow kerf and tolerances near $ \pm 0.1 $ mm, which suits thin sheet and complicated profiles. Additive accuracy depends on the process: FDM machines build layers 0.1 to 0.3 mm thick with dimensional errors of 0.2 to 0.5 mm, while stereolithography reaches about 50 µm at limited throughput.

## Volume economics and process capability

Volume capability is usually set by tooling cost and cycle time: processes with expensive moulds but low marginal cost suit large batches, whereas anything requiring fresh positioning for every part suits only small runs. In quality management, the process capability index $ C_{pk} \ge 1.33 $ is the usual gate for series production, since it means the tolerance band can absorb ±6σ of natural variation.

| Process | Typical volume | Unit cost | Accuracy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CNC milling | Hundreds to tens of thousands | Medium | ±0.01 mm |
| High-pressure die casting | Hundreds of thousands and up | Low | ±0.05 mm |
| Open-die forging | Tens of thousands | Medium | ±0.2 mm |
| Injection moulding | Hundreds of thousands to millions | Very low | ±0.05 mm |
| Stamping | Hundreds of thousands and up | Very low | ±0.03 mm |
| FDM additive manufacturing | Tens of parts | High | ±0.3 mm |

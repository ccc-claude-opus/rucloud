## Overview

A **weather system** is a relatively self-contained unit of the atmosphere that produces a particular kind of weather, ranging from a convective cell of a few hundred kilometres to a planetary wave spanning a hemisphere. They arise from contrasts between air masses and the lifting that occurs along their boundaries.

## Air masses and fronts

An air mass is a large body of air that has acquired broadly uniform properties over a wide region. When two air masses of very different character meet, they form a front: a cold front if cold air advances, a warm front if warm air glides up over the colder mass. The temperature gradient between the tropics and the poles is the driver, which is why the mid-latitudes host the most active weather.

## Highs and lows

Air rises around a low-pressure centre, cools and condenses into cloud and precipitation; around a high-pressure centre air sinks and the weather stays mostly clear. This hemispheric asymmetry is the origin of everyday rules like expecting rain when the pressure falls.

## Common systems

- Tropical cyclones: intense lows that form over warm ocean with a coherent structure, bringing strong wind and heavy rain.
- Extratropical cyclones: mid-scale systems combining a front with a low, a common source of rain in the mid-latitudes.
- Tornadoes: small rotating columns within severe thunderstorms, with wind speeds above $100$ m/s.

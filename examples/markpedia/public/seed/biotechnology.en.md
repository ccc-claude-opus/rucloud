## Overview

**Biotechnology** is the collection of techniques that harness biological systems or organisms to make products and solve problems, ranging from traditional fermentation to genetic engineering and synthetic biology. It spans agriculture, medicine, food and industry.

## Classification and history

Biotechnology is commonly split by field of application into red (health), green (agriculture), white (industrial) and grey (environmental). Humans have exploited microbes for thousands of years, as in yeast leavening for bread and beer and lactic acid bacteria in yogurt and dairy; the modern field begins with recombinant DNA in 1973 and the launch of recombinant insulin in 1982, which opened a new era for the pharmaceutical industry.

## Medical and agricultural applications

Biotechnology is now a principal engine of drug development, and both the mRNA vaccine platform of 2020 and the 2023 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine underline its importance while sharply lowering the cost of nucleic acid synthesis. In agriculture, tissue culture can multiply millions of orchid plantlets in a few months, while microbial insecticides and biofertilizers reduce reliance on chemical pesticides and synthetic fertilizer, and insect-resistant cotton has visibly cut insecticide applications in the field.

## Industry and energy

White biotechnology uses large-scale precision fermentation to make amino acids, vitamins, organic acids and enzymes. Fully fermented production of vitamin B2 and of some antibiotics has entirely displaced chemical synthesis, and biological routes remove synthetic steps, cutting energy use by $30$ % to $50$ %. In bioenergy, corn ethanol and waste-oil biodiesel account for the bulk of output, while microbial oils and syngas fermentation remain in development.

## Risk and governance

Applying biotechnology raises corresponding demands for risk management. Biosafety assessment of engineered crops, laboratory biosafety levels, the antimicrobial resistance question discussed alongside gene editing, and the capacity for designing organisms offered by synthetic biology are all regulatory concerns. Internationally these are handled through biosafety and biosecurity frameworks, with international treaties and import inspection requirements used to reconcile differing national review standards.

| Field | Representative technique | Typical application |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Red (health) | Gene therapy, cell therapy, mRNA vaccines | Vaccines, rare disease therapies |
| Green (agriculture) | Transgenesis, tissue culture, biopesticides | Insect-resistant crops, propagation |
| White (industry) | Precision fermentation, enzyme engineering | Amino acids, vitamins, biofuels |
| Grey (environment) | Wastewater treatment, compost microbes, biodegradation | Effluent treatment, plastic breakdown |

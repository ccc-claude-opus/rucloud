## Overview

**A school of philosophy** is a tradition formed by philosophers who share core commitments, methods, and lines of transmission. Identifying one usually requires looking at its doctrines, its method, its leading figures, and its institutional vehicle.

## What makes a school

A philosophical school usually has four elements: core theses about what is true, methods for arguing for them, a canon of texts, and an institutional vehicle such as a teaching post. Ideas lacking such a vehicle may still be called schools retrospectively, but organized transmission makes traditions more durable — the Academy founded by Plato in $387$ BCE lasted for centuries, and the Stoics took their name from the painted colonnade in Athens.

## Principal traditions

Chinese and Western traditions differ in origin and in the questions they took up. Chinese traditions center on self-cultivation and political order, while the Western classical line began with the first principles of the world and moved on to language, mind, and ethics. From the early modern period onward, numerous schools divided over experience, reason, and historical consciousness.

| Tradition | Central concern | Representative figures |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Confucianism | Ren, ritual, self-cultivation and government | Confucius, Mencius, Xunzi |
| Daoism | Non-action, nature, withdrawal from politics | Laozi, Zhuangzi |
| Ancient Greek philosophy | First principles and the good life | Socrates, Plato, Aristotle |
| Stoicism | Living according to nature | Seneca, Marcus Aurelius |
| Empiricism | Experience as the main source of knowledge | Bacon, Locke, Hume |
| Rationalism | Reason as the main source of knowledge | Descartes, Leibniz, Spinoza |
| Modern analytic philosophy | Language analysis and logical method | Russell, Wittgenstein |

## Organization and transmission

Chinese philosophy was transmitted largely through commentary on classics and academies, as when the Jixia academy in the Warring States state of Qi gathered contending schools and Xunzi served there three times as head of the academy; Western philosophy relied more on academies and chairs, with the Academy founded by Plato in $387$ BCE running for nearly $900$ years. Modern departments carry the tradition through journals, peer review, and degree systems, bringing otherwise separate schools into dialogue.

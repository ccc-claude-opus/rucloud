## Overview

**Novel** is a prose narrative form depicting characters and events across an extended span. It took shape in eighteenth-century print culture and now stands alongside poetry and drama as the most widely circulated narrative form, with long novels often running to hundreds of thousands of words.

## Narrative structure and point of view

Narrative effect depends mainly on temporal arrangement and the management of point of view. An omniscient narrator can enter any character’s mind; a restricted viewpoint deliberately withholds information so that readers share a character’s uncertainty; free indirect discourse blends the narrator’s voice with a character’s awareness and became a staple of twentieth-century fiction. Parallel plotlines let unrelated strands echo each other along the same timeline.

| Mode | Description | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chronological | Events narrated in order | 《左傳》 |
| Omniscient | Narrator knows all characters’ thoughts | 《安娜·卡列尼娜》 |
| Restricted | Only one character’s knowledge available | 《海邊的卡夫卡》 |
| Flashback and polyphony | Disordered time, several strands juxtaposed | 《追憶似水年華》, vol. 1 |

## Length and serialization

The scale of the novel has grown continuously since the eighteenth century, when a long novel ran to some $100,000$ characters, while modern titles often fall between $200,000$ and $400,000$ characters. Dickens published much of his work first in magazines and then in volume, and Chinese vernacular chapter novels organized themselves into numbered episodes — $120$ in 《三國演義》 alone.

## Genres and cycles

Novels divide by length or by convention. Detective fiction follows a fixed clue-and-solution structure, the historical novel reconstructs the past through researched detail, and science fiction extrapolates from imagined technical settings. Balzac planned $91$ volumes for the Human Comedy, a whole series set against a single society, and remains the most famous attempt at novelistic totality.

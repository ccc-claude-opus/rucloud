## Overview

**Historiography** studies how historians gather sources, judge their reliability, and construct explanations — the history of historical writing itself. It concerns texts, but also the institutions, moments, and values behind them.

## Sources and criticism

Sources divide into primary and secondary: primary materials were produced at the time, such as contracts, account books, inscriptions, and letters, while secondary sources are later accounts. The nineteenth-century German historian Leopold von Ranke argued for establishing history on critically sifted primary documents, an approach known as source-based history.

## Genres of historical writing

The same materials can be organized into very different narratives: annalistic writing records events year by year, biographical formats center on persons, and thematic history carries one question across periods. Genre choice determines which aspects come into view and which are passed over.

| Genre | Organization | Representative work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Annals | Events entered year by year | 《資治通鑑》 |
| Biographical | Centered on reigns, houses, and lives | 《史記》 |
| Fictionalized chronicle | Persons and stories string years together | 《三國演義》 |
| Thematic history | One question across periods | 《地中海與地中海世界》 |

## Diversifying method

Since the twentieth century historical method has split into divergent strands: the Annales school placed its centre of gravity on long-run economic and environmental structures, while quantitative history processed population, price, and election data with statistical and econometric models. Oral history treats memory itself as evidence, and gender history and global history examine groups absent from older narratives and the cross-regional links they had been neglecting. The Chinese 《史記》 fixed the biographical format in $130$ chapters.

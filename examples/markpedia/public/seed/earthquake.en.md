## Overview

An **earthquake** is the shaking of the ground caused by a sudden release of energy in the crust, most often from slip along a fault. Most are harmless, but a few great earthquakes can level a city and kill tens of thousands of people.

## How earthquakes happen

Rock accumulates stress as plates move until friction is overcome and the blocks slip suddenly, converting stored elastic strain into seismic waves. Quakes are classed as shallow (below $70$ km), intermediate ($70$ to $300$ km) and deep ($300$ to $700$ km) by source depth, and shallow events are usually most damaging, because the energy loses less intensity before reaching the surface.

## Magnitude and energy

Moment magnitude is computed from the seismic moment, and each unit increase multiplies the released energy by roughly $31.6$. The P wave travels at about $6$ to $8$ km/s and the S wave at $3.5$ to $4.5$ km/s, so the interval between them gives the distance to the source.

| Magnitude | Released energy | Typical effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $4$ | about $10^{12}$ J | Felt by many, little damage |
| $6$ | about $10^{14}$ J | Buildings damaged, walls cracked |
| $7$ | about $10^{15}$ J | Structures collapse, outside aid needed |
| $9$ | about $10^{18}$ J | Regional devastation, ground rupture |

## Records and warning

The largest instrumentally recorded earthquake was the $1960$ event in southern Chile, at magnitude $9.5$; the deadliest was the $1976$ Tangshan earthquake of magnitude about $7.6$, with an official death toll near $240000$ people. Taiwan's $1999$ Chi-Chi earthquake, magnitude $7.6$, also caused heavy losses. Dense networks and real-time systems now exploit the gap between early and late waves to buy seconds to tens of seconds of warning.

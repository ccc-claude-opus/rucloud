## Overview

**Epidemic control** comprises the public health measures that curb the spread of infectious disease. Its core task is to break the links between pathogen, transmission route and susceptible host, and to pick the interventions with the highest blocking efficiency when resources are scarce.

## Three links in the chain of infection

Transmission requires a pathogen, a route and a susceptible host, and control measures act respectively on the source, the route and the host. Modern control thinking adds the axes of person, place and time to define who, where and when must be isolated or traced. Because vaccines, testing capacity and isolation beds are all finite, the decision is essentially a comparison of blocking efficiency under a fixed budget.

## Non-pharmaceutical measures and their effects

Hand hygiene is among the cheapest and most reliable measures available; the CDC recommends scrubbing with soap for at least $20$ seconds, and hand rub with at least $60$ % alcohol works where running water is unavailable. Masks reduce inhalation of suspended particles, and when combined with ventilation and handwashing during respiratory outbreaks, absence attributed to respiratory infection among schoolchildren can fall by several percentage points. Environmental disinfection focuses on frequently touched surfaces such as door handles and touchscreens.

## Isolation, quarantine and vaccination

**Isolation** applies to people who are infected or diagnosed, whereas **quarantine** applies to exposed people who have no symptoms; both run for a period set by the pathogen's incubation and transmissibility, for instance isolating measles cases until at least $4$ days after rash onset. Vaccination is the most effective preventive tool, and because the basic reproduction number $R_0$ of measles is estimated at $12$ to $18$, roughly $95$ % vaccination coverage is needed to stop community transmission.

| Measure | Link addressed | Key point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hand hygiene | Interrupting transmission | Scrub for at least $20$ seconds |
| Mask use | Interrupting transmission | Crowded and high-risk settings |
| Isolation | Controlling the source | Duration set by route of spread |
| Quarantine | Interrupting transmission | Contact tracing and testing |
| Vaccination | Protecting susceptible hosts | Sustaining high coverage |
| Testing and surveillance | Early detection | Expanding testing capacity |

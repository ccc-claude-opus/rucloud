## Overview

A **food system** is the complete chain from production, storage, processing and retail through to consumption and waste. It shapes both what people eat and the environmental burden of agriculture, and a bottleneck anywhere in the chain shows up in food prices and hunger.

## Chain and scale

A food system runs through production, aggregation, processing, transport, retail, consumption and disposal, each stage supported by its own technologies and industries. Agriculture must feed roughly $8$ billion people, yet equipment is distributed very unevenly: only about $3.6$ million tractors and $1.7$ million combine harvesters are in worldwide use.

| Stage | Main activity | Common indicators |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Production | Cropping and livestock | Yield per hectare, total output |
| Processing and packaging | Milling, slaughtering, canning | Processing loss rate |
| Transport and retail | Storage, cold chain, logistics | Transport emissions, spoilage |
| Consumption | Household and institutional diets | Daily intake, dietary diversity |
| Disposal | Retail and consumer waste | Food waste and loss volumes |

## Losses and waste

Food loss is the most direct waste within the system. The Food and Agriculture Organization estimates that about $13$ % of food is lost between harvest and retail because of poor transport, processing and storage conditions, while a further $19$ % is wasted at retail and consumer level, adding up to roughly one third. Household waste concentrates in home cooking and food service, and in regions lacking cold chains and proper storage, post-harvest loss can exceed $30$ %, far above rates seen in high-income countries.

## The double burden of nutrition

The same system can produce both undernutrition and excess. In 2023 some $730$ million people were still undernourished, and among children under five about $148$ million were stunted and $45$ million were wasted; in the same year roughly $1$ in $8$ adults was living with obesity. The double burden coexists with unequal food distribution and oversupply of processed foods, so policy has to address distribution and demand at the same time rather than either alone.

## Sustainability and policy response

Food systems are also a major source of greenhouse gas emissions, with food supply chains including land-use change accounting for about $18$ % to $34$ % of global emissions. Policy tools include fortifying staple foods such as rice and wheat with vitamins and minerals, food labelling and nutrition education, cutting retail and consumer waste, and encouraging conservation practices such as cover crops and fallowing.

## Overview

**Fishery** is the industry that catches, farms, processes and distributes aquatic animals and plants. It supplies roughly one sixth of the animal protein eaten by humanity, and it is where the problems of overfishing and sustainable management converge.

## Structure of the industry

Fisheries divide into marine capture, inland capture and aquaculture, with processing covering freezing, canning, fishmeal and fish sauce. In 2022 world output of aquatic animals reached about $18.5$ million tonnes, of which capture fisheries supplied some $9.1$ million tonnes and aquaculture $9.44$ million tonnes, so farming overtook capture in overall scale for the first time. Asia accounts for roughly $90$ % of global aquaculture, and China is the largest producer in both farming and capture.

## State of the resources

Marine resources are not unlimited. The Food and Agriculture Organization estimates maximum sustainable production of marine fish at roughly $180$ million tonnes, and in 2021 about $37.7$ % of assessed stocks were fished at biologically unsustainable levels, with $62.3$ % still inside sustainable limits. Some $10$ million tonnes of catch a year is turned into fishmeal and fish oil, and reliance on small forage species transmits fishing pressure down to the base of the food web.

## Aquaculture techniques and species

The rapid growth of aquaculture rests on a small number of high-yield species such as whiteleg shrimp, common carp and salmon. Feed conversion ratios for farmed fish usually fall between $1.0$ and $1.6$, meaning roughly one kilogram of feed yields one kilogram of fish, while cattle need about $6$ to $8$ kilograms of feed for the same output. Cages, recirculating aquaculture systems and rice-fish culture are the dominant models; recirculating systems can cut water exchange by more than $95$ %, though disease transmission and antibiotic use still demand strict control.

## Sustainability and management

Fishery management deals simultaneously with total allowable catches, gear selectivity and quota systems. Marine protected areas now cover about $8$ % of the global ocean, still short of the target to protect $30$ % by 2030, and sustainability certification schemes such as MSC remain a limited share of output. Genuine progress requires moving from single-species total allowable catch towards ecosystem-based management of mixed fisheries and habitat protection.

## Overview

**Microeconomics** studies the choices of individuals and firms in markets, especially how prices guide the allocation of scarce resources. It explains why the same good carries different prices across markets and supplies the basic tools for demand forecasting, pricing and competitive analysis.

## The law of demand and elasticity

The law of demand says that, other things equal, quantity demanded falls as price rises, but the size of the response varies. Price elasticity is defined as $ E_d = -\frac{\%\Delta Q_d}{\%\Delta P} $; an absolute value above 1 means demand is elastic. If a 10 % price rise cuts coffee sales by 5 %, elasticity is 0.5, demand is inelastic, and the firm can convert the price rise into higher margin.

## Equilibrium and surplus

A market clears at the price $ P^{*} $ where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied. A price above equilibrium creates a surplus, one below creates a shortage, and both gaps push the price back toward balance. Consumer surplus is the total gap between willingness to pay and price actually paid; with linear demand its area is $ \frac{1}{2} \times (P_{max} - P^{*}) \times Q^{*} $, a standard measure of the welfare effect of a price change or subsidy.

## Main analytical tools

| Concept | Definition | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Marginal utility | Extra utility gained from one more unit | Explains why the demand curve slopes down |
| Opportunity cost | Highest value forgone by a choice | True cost of deploying a resource |
| Market structure | Perfect competition, monopoly, oligopoly, monopolistic competition | Determines pricing power and long-run profit |
| Consumer surplus | Difference between willingness to pay and price | Measures welfare loss from taxes or monopoly |

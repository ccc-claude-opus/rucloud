## Overview

**Civilization** is a view of history centered on the long-term accumulation of cultural achievement, asking how institutions, technologies, and ideas took shape and were sustained. Its subject is cumulative effect across generations rather than any single event.

## Criteria

Archaeology and history usually identify civilization by several criteria together: concentration of population into cities, class differentiation with specialized rulers, a system of written record, metalworking, and long-distance exchange. The criteria do not arrive at once — Mesopotamian cities and temples existed by about $3500$ BCE, well before writing was used.

## Early civilizations

Several distinct civilizational cores formed separately across the Old and New Worlds, each developing its own form of state and script. They are not branches of one spreading culture but independent points of origin, which is central to the modern revision of older diffusionist accounts; even for the later civilizations of the Americas, whether they derive from Asia, the Pacific, or an independent development remains disputed.

| Civilization | Core region | Approximate rise | Distinguishing feature |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sumer | Mesopotamia | 3500 BCE | Cuneiform on clay, city-states |
| Ancient Egypt | Nile valley | 3100 BCE | Royal tombs, temples, dynasties |
| Harappa | Indus valley | 2600 BCE | Grid streets and drainage |
| Erlitou | Yellow River valley | 1750 BCE | Bronze ritual vessels, palace foundations |
| Olmec | Gulf coast of Mexico | 1200 BCE | Colossal stone heads |

## Long-run change in numbers

Civilization history can also be checked against data. Over the past century and a half urbanization, life expectancy, literacy, and output per person all rose sharply, yet the gaps between regions remain large, showing that accumulation is not synchronous. Literacy in particular exposes differences in pace: some countries moved from 20 % to above 90 % within a few decades, while others made almost no progress over the same period.

| Indicator | c. 1900 | c. 2020 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Share of world population urban | 13 % | 56 % |
| Global life expectancy at birth | 32 years | 72 years |
| Adult literacy rate | 20 % | 87 % |
| Global GDP per capita (PPP) | about 2,500 USD | about 17,000 USD |

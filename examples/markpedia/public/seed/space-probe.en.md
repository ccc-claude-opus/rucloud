## Overview

A **space probe** is an unmanned spacecraft sent into space to gather data from environments beyond the protection of Earth's magnetosphere. Unlike a satellite, a probe is usually a single-mission platform — it can be reduced to a sensor package, and even crash into its target after taking samples.

## Phases of a planetary mission

A typical planetary mission runs through launch and orbit injection, cruise, trajectory correction, approach and final insertion, then the science phase. Each phase needs a different propulsion strategy, and approach is especially demanding for navigation because radar ranging and imaging both carry delay.

## Representative missions

| Probe | Target | Distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Voyager 1 | Jupiter, Saturn | Launched 1977, still in interstellar space |
| Cassini | Saturn | Carried the Huygens lander down to Titan |
| Curiosity | Mars | Nuclear-powered mobile laboratory |
| New Horizons | Pluto | Flyby followed by exploration of the Kuiper belt |

## The difficulty of atmospheric entry

Entering a dense atmosphere, a probe must go from thousands of times the speed of sound to rest in minutes while surviving aerodynamic heating. The usual answer combines a heat shield, parachutes and thrusters; in 2020 Soyuz 11 became the first craft to land on another planet using powered descent.

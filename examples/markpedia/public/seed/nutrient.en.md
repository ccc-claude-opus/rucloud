## Overview

A **nutrient** is a component of food the body needs to maintain physiological function, divided into macronutrients that supply energy and micronutrients that regulate processes. The amounts, ratios and sources of these together shape health.

## The six classes of nutrients

Nutrients fall into **macronutrients** (carbohydrate, protein and fat), **micronutrients** (vitamins and minerals) and water, six classes in all. Macronutrients supply both energy and structure, whereas micronutrients take part in enzymatic reactions, hormone synthesis and antioxidant defence but are needed only in milligram or microgram amounts. The body cannot synthesise most vitamins, so they must come from food, and sustained shortages produce characteristic deficiency diseases such as scurvy and beriberi.

## Energy conversion and estimating requirements

The energy densities of the macronutrients are $4$ kcal/g for carbohydrate and protein, $9$ kcal/g for fat and about $7$ kcal/g for alcohol, so an equal weight of fat supplies roughly $2.25$ times the energy of carbohydrate. Daily energy needs can be estimated as basal metabolic rate times an activity factor, with the Harris-Benedict equation for men given by $BMR = 66.47 + 13.75W + 5.003H - 6.755A$; sedentary activity factors are about $1.2$.

| Nutrient | Main role | Approximate adult daily intake |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Energy | Activity and temperature maintenance | about $1\,600$ to $2\,000$ kcal |
| Protein | Tissue repair and enzyme synthesis | about $0.8$ to $1.0$ g per kg body weight |
| Fibre | Bowel motility and metabolic control | about $25$ to $38$ g |
| Calcium | Bone mineralisation and nerve conduction | about $1\,000$ mg |
| Iron | Haemoglobin synthesis and oxygen transport | about $8$ mg for men, $18$ mg for women |
| Vitamin D | Calcium absorption and immune regulation | about $10$ mcg ($400$ IU) |
| Water | Fluid balance and metabolic clearance | about $1\,500$ to $2\,000$ mL |

## Deficiency and excess

Both deficiency and excess are harmful: iron overload generates oxidative stress and organ injury, chronic excess of vitamin A can cost bone density, and shortfalls in several nutrients at once slow wound healing and weaken immunity. Pregnant women, older adults and vegetarians form the highest-risk groups, so supplements should rest on dietary assessment and blood results rather than on impression. Nutrients also compete for absorption, and taking large amounts of calcium and iron together reduces the efficiency of both.

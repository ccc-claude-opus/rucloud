## Overview

**Ethnicity** refers to groups sharing culture, language or historical experience, and to the social boundaries that distinguish them from others. It is the starting point of research on ethnic minorities and a framework for understanding conflict, identity and inequality.

## Ethnicity and race

Race and ethnicity are often conflated, but scholarship generally treats race as an earlier social construction tied to slavery, colonialism and racial science, while ethnicity rests on shared cultural features and self-identification. In 1969 Fredrik Barth's Ethnic Groups and Boundaries argued that study should focus on how boundaries are maintained and transformed across contact — cultural content may change while the boundary persists — rather than treating ethnic groups as internally uniform and fixed.

## Constructivist and instrumental approaches

Two explanatory orientations diverge sharply. Constructivists, such as Rogers Brubaker, argue that ethnic boundaries are continually produced by institutions like schools, markets and the state, and stress their malleability; instrumentalists hold that elites selectively amplify ethnic differences to mobilise support in competition over resources. The two are not exclusive: in some settings boundaries are constructed, and in others the construction itself serves a defined political interest.

## Language and displacement

Language is the most frequently cited marker of ethnic identity, and UNESCO's atlas of endangered languages lists about 40 % of the world's languages as endangered, most projected to disappear by the end of the century unless trends reverse. Forced migration reshapes ethnic geography: UNHCR data put the number of forcibly displaced people at about 122 million in mid-2024, the great majority of them displaced within their own countries.

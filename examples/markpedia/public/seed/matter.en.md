## Overview

**Matter** is anything that possesses mass and occupies space, forming the basic material of the known universe. All matter is built from chemical elements, and on a macroscopic scale it appears as solid, liquid, gas or plasma.

## Levels of organisation

Matter divides into elements, compounds and mixtures. An element is made of neutral atoms sharing the same atomic number and cannot be broken down chemically, while a compound combines two or more elements in fixed proportions. In a mixture the proportions vary and components can be separated by physical means such as distillation, extraction or filtration. Oxygen accounts for about $60$ % to $70$ % of adult body mass, carbon about $18$ % and hydrogen about $10$ %.

## Mass, density and measurement

Mass does not change with location, whereas density is defined by the relation $\rho = m / V$ for a body of fixed mass. Water has a density of about $1.000$ g/cm³ at $4$ °C, gold about $19.3$ g/cm³, hydrogen only $0.0000899$ g/cm³, and air roughly $1.225$ kg/m³. Sorting by density separates matter broadly into solids, liquids and gases, with the densest element, osmium, reaching about $22.6$ g/cm³.

## Phases and phase changes

A phase reflects how particles are arranged and how free they are to move, which in turn fixes density and compressibility.

| Phase | Particle arrangement | Volume behaviour | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Solid | Fixed lattice | Almost unchanged by compression | Ice, iron |
| Liquid | Closely packed but mobile | Takes the shape of the container | Water, alcohol |
| Gas | Sparse, random motion | Varies with pressure and temperature | Air, helium |
| Plasma | Free charged particles | Affected by electric and magnetic fields | Solar corona, neon tube |

Phase changes require latent heat, as melting ice absorbs about $334$ J/g, and water boils at $100$ °C at 1 atm with a latent heat of vaporisation near $2260$ J/g, so evaporation costs more energy than merely warming the same water.

## The unity of matter and fields

In modern physics matter and fields are no longer opposed categories but two descriptions of the same thing: particles are viewed as excitations of quantum fields, and quarks and photons are the elementary field quanta of the Standard Model. This picture lets the behaviour of matter be described within a unified field-theoretic framework and explains why particles can be created and annihilated.

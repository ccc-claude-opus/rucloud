## Overview

**Marketing** is the activity of identifying customer needs and creating, communicating and delivering value. It goes beyond promotion to cover the whole mix of product, price, place and customer relationships.

## Segmentation and positioning

Marketing planning usually begins with segmentation — splitting the market along geographic, demographic, psychographic and behavioural variables — before selecting target segments and defining a position. Positioning in practice means a distinctive value proposition relative to rivals, built on price, specification, service speed or proprietary technology. Segments that are too small lack scale; those that are too broad give no basis for a communication programme, which matters when global advertising spending of about 800 billion US dollars in 2023 is already more than half digital.

## The marketing mix and its metrics

The marketing mix covers product, price, place and promotion. Performance is tracked with measures such as click-through rate, conversion rate and customer acquisition cost: average e-commerce checkout conversion sits between 2 % and 3 %. Customer lifetime value is often approximated by $ CLV = \frac{AOV \times f \times m}{c} $, with $ AOV $ the average order value, $ f $ the annual purchase frequency, $ m $ the gross margin and $ c $ the annual churn rate.

## Composition of the mix

| Element | Core question | Common tools |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product | What is sold and what problem does it solve? | Features, branding, service experience |
| Price | How much is charged and how? | Pricing strategy, discounts, payment terms |
| Place | Where is it delivered? | Physical stores, e-commerce, distributors |
| Promotion | How do target customers find out? | Advertising, public relations, events, content |

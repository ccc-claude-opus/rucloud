## Overview

**Business law** governs commercial conduct, the formation of contracts, corporate governance and competition. It supplies the minimum rules that make transactions predictable and allow disputes to be resolved at manageable cost.

## Formation and effect of contracts

A contract requires an offer and an acceptance, and an acceptance takes effect on reaching the offeror. Where a standard-form clause violates the requirement of conspicuous fairness, consumers may rely on Article 12 of the Consumer Protection Act, in force since 13 January 1994, to void the clause or demand its revision. Performance is governed by full-performance and good-faith principles, and breach entails liability in damages.

## Corporate organisation and governance

The Company Act sets out the powers of the shareholders' meeting and the board, while company registration lets counterparties verify that an entity exists. The amendment effective 1 May 2009 removed minimum capital requirements for limited companies, and a further revision on 1 November 2018 strengthened the board's role and introduced electronic voting. The Fair Trade Act also imposes a prior notification duty on mergers at thresholds of $ \frac{1}{4} $ in the relevant market or above $ \frac{1}{2} $ after merging.

## Main fields of business law

| Field | Core question | Representative statutes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contracts | Is the promise binding, and how is breach compensated? | Civil Code |
| Corporate organisation | Incorporation, governance and director liability | Company Act |
| Competition | Monopoly and restrictive practices | Fair Trade Act |
| Consumer protection | Standard-form terms, defects and recalls | Consumer Protection Act |
| Intellectual property | Trademarks, patents and trade secrets | Trademark Act, Patent Act, Copyright Act |
| Financial supervision | Fundraising, trading and disclosure duties | Securities Act, Financial Supervisory Commission Act |

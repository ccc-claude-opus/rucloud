## Overview

**Securities** are financial instruments representing property rights or claims to payment, such as shares, bonds and fund units. They connect borrowers needing funds with investors seeking returns, and they are the basic instruments of capital markets.

## Main categories and the rights they carry

A share represents ownership in the issuing company, with dividends and votes allocated in proportion to holdings; a bond is a claim on the issuer carrying a stated coupon and a repayment date; a fund unit pools investors' money for professional management. The ownership–claim distinction determines who is paid first: dividends are discretionary, whereas interest on a bond is a contractual obligation.

## Return, risk and pricing

The required return on an asset can be written with the capital asset pricing model as $ E(R_i) = R_f + \beta_i (E(R_m) - R_f) $, where $ \beta_i $ measures systematic risk relative to the market and $ R_f $ is the risk-free rate. A bond's yield to maturity is approximated by $ y = \frac{C + (F - P)/n}{(F + P)/2} $, with $ C $ the coupon, $ F $ the face value, $ P $ the price and $ n $ the number of coupon periods.

## Market size and structure

| Market | Size in 2023 | Feature |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Global equity markets | about 107 trillion USD | Source of equity capital and shareholder returns |
| Global bond markets | about 133 trillion USD | The main venue for fixed income and interest-rate risk |
| Centralised exchanges | Order-driven, transparent prices | Higher liquidity and price transparency |
| Over-the-counter markets | Negotiated, dealer-quoted | Suits smaller issuers |
| Funds | Pooled investment | Diversification with tradable units |

## Overview

**Marine ecology** studies how marine organisms interact with one another and with the ocean environment. Covering about $71$ % of the Earth's surface, the ocean is the largest and least easily observed part of the biosphere.

## Conditions in the ocean

Average seawater salinity is about $35$ ‰, far higher than freshwater, and temperature falls rapidly with depth, with deep water often holding steady at $2$ to $4$ °C. Because light penetrates only so far, the photosynthetic zone rarely extends below $200$ m, beneath which lies the permanently dark deep sea.

| Layer | Depth | Light | Production and typical life |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sunlit zone | $0$–$200$ m | Enough for photosynthesis | Phytoplankton, corals, schooling fish |
| Twilight zone | $200$–$1000$ m | Faint | Bioluminescent organisms, migrating zooplankton |
| Deep sea | $1000$–$4000$ m | Total darkness | Deep-sea fish, sea cucumbers, bacteria |
| Abyssal zone | below $4000$ m | Total darkness | Trench floors, chemosynthetic communities |

## Productivity and food webs

Phytoplankton such as diatoms and dinoflagellates carry out most of the ocean's primary production, contributing roughly $48.5$ Gt of carbon a year, comparable with land. About $90$ % of energy is lost at each step up the food chain, so fish, sharks and seabirds occupy only a sliver of the original energy, which is why marine stocks recover so slowly after overfishing.

## The deep sea and conservation

The deep sea is not lifeless: hydrothermal vents found on the East Pacific Rise in $1977$ revealed a complete ecosystem powered by chemosynthesis rather than sunlight. Coral reefs cover less than $0.1$ % of the seafloor yet shelter roughly a quarter of marine species, and they are among the systems most damaged by warming and acidification. Marine protected areas currently cover about $8$ % of the ocean, most of it near coasts and around hotspots.

## Overview

**Algebra** is the branch of mathematics that represents quantities with symbols and manipulates algebraic expressions under fixed rules of operation. It grew out of practical equation solving into the abstract study of structures such as groups, rings and fields.

## Symbolic manipulation and solving equations

The basic task of algebra is to replace concrete numbers with symbols and manipulate expressions under stated axioms. The roots of the quadratic equation $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ are given by the quadratic formula:

$$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$

The discriminant $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac$ decides the outcome, where $\Delta > 0$ gives two distinct real roots, $\Delta = 0$ a repeated root, and $\Delta < 0$ a conjugate pair of complex roots. In practice, substitution lowers the difficulty of higher-degree equations, since setting $t = x + 1/x$ reduces a quartic to a quadratic.

## Algebraic structures and the fundamental theorem

Twentieth-century algebra shifted from solving equations to studying the structure of operation systems themselves. The fundamental theorem of algebra states that a polynomial of degree $n$ over the complex numbers has exactly $n$ roots counted with multiplicity, so every polynomial factors into linear terms. The main algebraic structures differ as follows.

| Structure | Defining feature | Typical example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Group | One operation, every element invertible | Integers under addition |
| Ring | Two operations, multiplication distributes | Integer ring, polynomial ring |
| Field | Ring where every nonzero element has an inverse | Rationals, reals, complex numbers |
| Module | Ring with a vector space structure over a field | Real coordinate space in $n$ dimensions |

## Symmetry and links to other disciplines

Algebra is the natural language of symmetry. In quantum mechanics, unitary transformations of matrices encode exchange symmetry of particles, while group theory classifies molecular symmetry into point groups such as $C_{nv}$ and similar families. Conservation laws are likewise explained algebraically, since angular momentum satisfies the commutation relation $[L_x, L_y] = i\hbar L_z$ with $\hbar \approx 1.0546 \times 10^{-34}$ J·s. Symbolic systems such as Mathematica, SymPy and SageMath all run algebraic algorithms, from polynomial factorization to Gröbner basis computation.
